## Production shortfalls

**Merged the shortfall session's fixes** (`claude/brave-yonath-05f458`): the validator models split producers (a
machine putting one item on two belt chains divides its output by inserter speed, not as planned) and the router
avoids them; crackers obey their circuit condition in game (`circuit_enabled`, read through a wired pipe). The
validator's lane-true producer split (newer, on this branch) and the split-producer shares now run in one max flow.

**The in-game verifier takes outputs at the planned rate by default** (`--uncapped` for the old behaviour), so a
factory cannot look good by over-making one pack while starving another. `--dump` adds every belt's lanes, inserter
and pipe to the report, and `tools/factorio-verify/starve.py <out>` traces each starving machine: the belts that
bring the missing item, upstream to who makes it, who else takes it on the way, and whether a producer standing
with full output can reach it at all.

**Why production science stayed at 1.81/s** in the re-solved 2/s factory (traced with starve.py on the shortfall
session's last in-game run): the two production-science machines at the end of the rail belt starve for rails,
while none of the four rail assemblers is blocked. Rails are short because the plan runs the rail and iron-stick
machines at exactly 100% of their speed (4 rail assemblers for exactly 10 crafts/s, 2 stick assemblers for exactly
5): any lost inserter swing upstream is lost for good. Block rebalancing also pushed machines to 100% (the
electric-furnace assemblers at 0.25/s of 0.25).

- New `planner.max_utilization`: machine counts round up past it and block rebalancing stops there. Default 0.97
  (see the community jobs below). The 2/s factory was re-solved with 0.97 overnight (`solves/two_sps_headroom`,
  1,261 machines against 1,220).

The first three were the split producers (fixed by the merge). Green 1/s is the 100% case; **max_utilization 0.97 is
now the default**. Tests that check hand-worked machine counts (and fixtures placed for them) use `handTuning`
(max_utilization 1). Green 22.5/s with beacons is a different fault: every machine is 20–30% short of ingredients
while the input belts are offered in full. The beaconed assemblers (16 crafts/s) are fed by one bulk inserter per
ingredient from belts that are not backed up; the inserter model (2.31 swings/s x hand 12 x belt derate 0.7 =
19.4/s) looks optimistic there.

**Bulk inserters from belts, measured.** One beaconed gear assembler fed by one bulk inserter (hand 12) from a full
express belt, outputs taken without limit: the inserter delivers 13.5 iron/s whatever the machine wants (19.4 in the
model). A bulk inserter waits for its hand to fill from the passing items: one swing plus 12 items at 0.58 of the
belt's throughput fits. With that in the inserter model the same job gets two inserters and makes its plan, and
green 22.5/s holds 22.5/s. Plain fast inserters are the other way round: one at hand 1 took 2.73/s from a belt (1.62
in the model), so the 0.7 belt derate is conservative for them (left as it is).

**Split producers at full speed.** The validator held a split producer at its planned rate and divided that by
inserter speed. In the game a machine whose outputs are all taken runs faster, so the division only bites at full
speed. With headroom every machine has spare speed, and the old model rejected sound factories (a gear assembler
feeding its own block and another one). The split now uses full speed as the machine's ceiling.

**Ring overlaps in factories.** Compaction could squeeze a block side to no padding while the block had heat pipes
on its ring, so two blocks' ring pipes landed on one tile (HeatTest failed now and then, OVERLAP or COLD). Sides
with anything on the ring keep a tile.

**Lane overflow in six-science factories** (the error the 2sc_job_2 resume died on, and the 2/s preview stitches
tonight): the validator's own fallback. When the lane-true producer split could not meet every draw of any one
item with producers at their planned rates, every item fell back to an arbitrary split, which piled one lane full
(38/s on a 30/s lane). Producers may now run up to full speed there, and only items without a lane-true split fall
back. The 2/s preview that failed with 12 overflow errors validates.

## Block ports on the wrong edge

Found in 9 of 10 factory runs in `solves/` (e.g. iron ore on the north edge of a job that says west). Cause: the
floorplan may turn a block, and a port that leaves the factory straight out of the block turns with it. A block
with such ports now only takes turns that keep each of them on an edge the job allows.

## Factory packing

In the 2/s factory's stitch, blocks cover 59% of the box; of the other 20,481 tiles only 4,030 hold anything.
Whole padding rows hold nothing but poles, and there are large empty pockets (the sequence-pair packing's slack).
The squeeze keeps a change only if the factory shrinks, and each try re-routes everything, so it is time-bound.
This is the biggest lever left on factory size.

Seam carving (taking out a staircase line of empty or straight-belt tiles that weaves around blocks, where no whole
row is free) was prototyped on the 2/s preview: 2 rows and 1 column, about 1%. Corridor belts cross almost every
seam. Not built.

What starves (`starve.py`): chemical science waits for engines, engines for steel; production science for electric
furnaces; utility for processing units, processing units for advanced circuits. In each case every machine that
feeds the starving one runs at 100%, while machines of the same recipe in other blocks stand with full output, their

Why: every stage is planned at 97%, so a supplier flat out only just covers a consumer flat out. As soon as anything

another block cannot help. **Next step:** more headroom upstream than downstream, e.g. utilization 0.97 for the
targets and a little lower per level up the chain (smelting the most), or links sized for the consumers' full speed.
Needs a full re-solve (about 5 hours) to check in game.

## More headroom up the chain

New `planner.upstream_utilization` (default 1, off): a recipe's machines are planned at `max_utilization ×
upstream_utilization^level`, where its level is its longest way down to a target, so smelting gets the most spare.
`solves/two_sps_upstream` (0.97 and 0.98): 1,366 machines, 56 blocks, 217 x 219 = 47,523 tiles (5% more than the
headroom-only factory). In game, same check as above:

| pack | headroom only | headroom up the chain |
|---|---|---|
| automation | 2 | 1.87 |
| logistic, military | 2 | 2 |
| chemical | 1.74 | **2** |
| production | 1.93 | **2** |
| utility | 1.88 | **2** |

The three that starved are fixed. Automation science lost: one of its nine machines is short of gears. The gear
block (4 assemblers, 7.67/s planned, 10/s at full speed) sends gears out on two belts, 5/s and 2.67/s, and the 5/s
belt is fed by exactly two assemblers, 5/s flat out. All the block's spare speed sits on the other belt. The
inserter and belt assemblers upstream on the 5/s belt take a little more than planned and the last science machine
goes without. The validator allowed that because it lets producers run at full speed to fill a belt.

**Validator: intermediates up to max_utilization, not flat out.** A machine making an intermediate may now run up
to `max(planned, max_utilization × full speed)` in the validator, so a belt that only producers at 100% can fill is
rejected and the search has to give it another producer (the 2/s gear block: 11 x 11 with three assemblers on the
5/s belt, against 10 x 10). Two stricter versions failed: the per-recipe utilization (0.895 for gears) made almost
every block of the 2/s factory fail, and holding target machines to it too left green 1/s and red + green 1.5/s
with no layout at all (13 science assemblers on two 0.5/s output ports: one port gets six, 0.485/s). Machines
making targets may run flat out (the edge ports are interchangeable). Community jobs with it, 5 minutes on 5
threads, in game: green 1/s 27 x 26, red + green 1.5/s 34 x 34, circuits 12/s 29 x 29, green 22.5/s 55 x 57, all
at plan.

**The 2/s factory with the validator ceiling** (`solves/two_sps_ceiling`, same plan as `two_sps_upstream`): 216 x 220,
valid, every port fed. In game: automation, logistic, military and chemical 2, **production 1.90, utility 1.57**.
Gears are fine now; a different link broke. In-game crafts per recipe against the plan: nothing runs above plan
(iron plate 91%, circuits 86%), so no stage over-draws in total. The shortage is distribution again: an iron chain
runs through four blocks (block 10 → 30 → 32 → 35, 20.28/s planned) and block 10's 37 furnaces run flat out (23.1/s)
while block 18's furnaces, whose belt joins block 32 too, stand with full output. Circuits in block 35 at the end of
the chain starve, then advanced circuits, processing units and utility science. The validator sees each block's
input ports as fixed supplies and the factory as valid; in game, when a consumer at the end starves, the machines
sharing its output belt speed up to cover, draw more from their own supply, and the chain drifts.

Three re-solves of the same factory, three different weak links (chemical, production and utility; automation;
production and utility). Plan counts are not the problem any more; long chains through several blocks are.
Candidates: links straight from smelting blocks to consumers (no pass-through of bulk plates), or a factory-level
check that lets every consumer draw up to its own headroom and fails where a chain cannot keep up.

## Why the chains do not level out (traced in game)

`tools/factorio-verify/chains.py <out> <item>` groups an item's producers and consumers by the belts that join
them in game and compares each with the plan. On `two_sps_ceiling` every iron belt is one network (406 furnaces, 274
consumers, 25 blocks: plates pass from block to block). Furnace blocks 15 and 18 stand mostly full (9.9 and 8.6 of
23.1 plates/s): their belts lead only to steel blocks 29 and 32, which are backed up because the rail and
electric-furnace assemblers that take steel from several steel blocks draw from the nearest first. Steel blocks 28
and 30 run flat out instead, and they pull extra plates from a chain they share with block 35's circuits, which
starve at its end. No misplaced inserter: spare capacity sits on belts that never reach the starving machines,
and the validator assumes a split the game does not make. The job had `blocks.pass_through` on (plates passing
through several blocks); re-solving with it off (`solves/two_sps_nopass`, each furnace block linked straight to
its users).

## Packing: two ideas that did not help

On six science at 0.5/s (331 machines, 17 blocks, 30 minutes each, one thread): leaving the blocks' own poles out
of the one-tile ring kept around a block (stitching re-places poles anyway), and letting neighbours share their
padding (the gap the larger of the two, not the sum). The code before: 110 x 113 (12,430 tiles); the pole fix:
115 x 114 (13,110); both: 108 x 120 (12,960). Neither kept.

## The 2/s factory without pass-through, in game

`solves/two_sps_nopass` (`blocks.pass_through` off, block search cut to 50 minutes): 207 x 227 = 46,989 tiles. In
game: automation, logistic, military, production 2; **chemical 0.92, utility 1.49**. Worse than with pass-through.
Steel block 25 gets 13.3 iron plates/s of its 20.1: its supplier (block 19, flat out at 23.1) also feeds a 0.15/s link
to steel block 29 off the same belt through a splitter, and a splitter gives up to half of the belt to a side that
takes it. The validator lets a splitter divide as the plan needs. Next: model the splitter's halves, or avoid tiny
side links.

## Blocks that could not be laid out (2sc_job_2)

Two causes. (1) The validator's headroom for intermediates (above, from this morning): 19 advanced-circuit
assemblers for belts of 8.79 and 4.76/s need 20 at 97% of full speed; three blocks never got a layout. The check
is now opt-in, `planner.headroom_check`. (2) A block whose machines cannot feed the belts an item leaves on, at any
speed: 5 circuit assemblers (8.82/s at most each) for belts of 19.36, 19.93 and 3.51/s. The split now adds copies of
the producer where a block needs them (counting half machines: a machine dropping on two belts gives each about
half), and the block's machines share its work: 8 more machines on that job (of about 1,500), every block laid out
in 77 s. A resumed run takes the headroom setting from the tuning and accepts a split with added machines.
