## The failing factory (not solved yet)

Py red science at 3/s plans the Py way (iron from pyrite: sulfur + phosphate rock -> P2S5 -> pyrite -> iron oxide,
with 69/s H2S and 28/s phosphoric acid leaving). It validates and builds, but in game it jams within minutes:
block 2's planter boxes starve of ash while the ash furnace stands with full output and ash is on the belt (looks
like a lane or belt-throughput problem on the ash belt); block 2 then stops taking iron, the smelter feeding it
backs up, the shared iron-oxide supply backs up, the pyrite burners stop, so acid gas and sulfur stop, and copper
(screeners and crushers with two products each) seizes too. One jam anywhere stops this tightly coupled chain.

Next: trace the ash belt (lanes, the inserters' pickup lanes) with `--dump` and `chains.py ash`; check the mirrored
HPFs' fluid connections (Py machines use mirroring; acid-gas pipes were nearly empty); then go up in size again.

## Morning: the jam found

Not ash, as it looked. Block 3's copper screeners and crushers give stone besides copper; it went to block 2's
gravel crushers, which also took raw stone from the edge. One crusher took from both belts, and both crushers drop
gravel on one belt: with raw stone always there, the by-product only got in when the raw side left a gap. Whenever block
2 needed less than both brought, block 3's stone backed up, the crushers and screeners stood with full output, copper
stopped, small parts and science stopped, and science stopped taking block 2's planter boxes: a loop that settled at
3% of the plan.

- **Raw by-products leave** (`plan/Byproducts.kt`, job option `raw_byproducts`, default "leave"): a raw resource a
  recipe makes besides other products becomes "name#byproduct", which nothing uses: it leaves as surplus and what
  needs the resource takes it raw. ("use" keeps the old behaviour.)
- **Planner rounds from scratch**: GLOP restarting from the previous round's basis came back "abnormal" in the first
  round on this plan (and stalled 20 s in the next), so the 650-sliver first solution was kept (672 machines). Each
  round now resets the solver (under a second each); weights are capped at 1,000 times a machine's area. Py red 3/s:
  61 machines, 20 recipes (copper now from plain smelting).
- Infinity chests in test copies got the game's name for their filter (quality and `#` names).
- verify.py matches ports by the game's name.

In game: 76% of plan, no jam. The one shortfall: the ash furnace's single bulk inserter.

- **Bulk inserters drop onto belts slower** (measured with a bench blueprint, 2.1, hand 12): 13.75/s onto express,
  11.0 onto fast (= 12 / (1/2.31 + 12 / (0.607 x belt))); a fast inserter keeps 6.9. The planner assumed 22/s.
  `inserterRate(..., toBelt)` and `BULK_DROP_SHARE`; used for drops onto belts (router, templates, test-copy feeders,
  validator).

In game after it: **93.1%** (2.79 of 3/s; 95% passes). What is left: three pyrite furnaces' iron oxide on one belt
(two bulk inserters on one lane measured 20.65/s, the router allows a lane 22.05).

## Py science pack 1 at 0.05/s (58 machines, 3 blocks): dead in game

Every machine idle. Found and fixed on the way:
- **Production loops** (fertilizer -> bones -> carcasses -> substrate -> moss -> limestone -> seaweed -> fertilizer):
  the planner names seeds (`plan.json` "seeds": carbon dioxide, fertilizer, formamide, sap trees, slacked lime, warm
  stone bricks); the verify mod puts 5 crafts' worth into their machines. Machines then craft during warm-up.
- The verify mod primed "catalysts" by game name at the default temperature: Py's warmer-stone-brick takes coke oven
  gas at 500 and gives it at 250, so it got cold gas and stood "input fluid too cold" forever. Now primed at the
  product's temperature, and only if the recipe takes that.
- verify.py held by-product outputs to their planned rate too: tar distillation, ahead of the plan at start, stopped
  on its 0.02/s rich clay. Now only targets are held.

Still dead: tar distillation's carbon dioxide (and other loops' by-products) back up while the loops they feed cannot
run yet, which stops the machines the loops need. Next: an overflow for by-products used inside (they also leave, or
are voided, when their users do not take them), as Py players vent gases.
