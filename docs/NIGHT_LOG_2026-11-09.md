## Morning: the corridor errors were unrouted 0/s links

Every stitch of Py science 2 and logistic since the overflow changes ended in one to eight validation errors in the
corridors, a different place each try but always two items: a SHAPE_CHANGED (a block's port belt feeding a corridor
belt from behind or the side) or two items on one lane. The invalid factory's report now lists what the stitch put
around each error, and it showed the same thing every time: the start tile of a link from a block to the factory's
edge (sea-sponge-sprouts, fiber, sodium-sulfate, guts) with another net's belt on it and none of its own.

Those links carry a block's overflow at 0/s. The router dropped 0/s input terminals (only 0/s outputs were kept, as
overflows), so a 0/s link lost its source, the net had nothing to start from, and the router skipped it while still
reporting success: no belt, and its first tile free for any other net. Where nothing ran over it, the overflow
dead-ended one tile out of its block (logistic's valid 299 x 342 factory from a re-stitch: 0/s of science in game, 208
machines finished nothing, many standing full). Single-belt (link) input ports now stay at 0/s and get their chain to
the overflow; a router test covers it.

With the 0/s links routed, logistic lays out valid at the first stitch (333 x 342, 4 minutes), but still made no
science in game: 189 machines finished nothing, while coke, borax and worms left through overflows (their users stood
for other reasons). The shortage trace led to one machine: the slaughterhouse (full-render-scrondrixs) stood with a
full output of pineal glands, which nothing uses and which go out on the leftovers' mixed belt; without it no blood,
meat or brain, and the rest of the factory starved behind it. Its inserter onto the mixed belt was in the blueprint
but not in the game: it had a blacklist of the slaughterhouse's 7 other products, and Py's long-handed inserter has
3 filter slots (inserter 2, fast 4, bulk 5; vanilla 2.1 gives every inserter 5). The game leaves such an inserter
out. Now:

- A machine with one product for the mixed belt gets a filter of that product instead of a blacklist of the others.
- An inserter that needs more filters than its type has is swapped for an allowed one of the same reach that has
  enough (the exporter's filter counts are used now; they were ignored for inserters the program knows by name).
- The validator reports an inserter with more filters than slots.

That first version broke every Py block with leftovers (logistic: 5 of 12 blocks without a layout after 15 minutes,
caught only on the next run): the filter used the plan's name ("stone#byproduct", which the game does not know), and
the validator, which counts a machine's mixed-belt products as one product named after the belt, called each such
inserter a wrong item. Filters now carry the game's names (blacklists had the same problem), and a machine's spec
maps its mixed-belt products to the belt's product. A validator test covers the case; logistic's blocks all lay out
in 44 s again.

With the leftover inserter built, the slaughterhouse ran (blood, meat, brain) and fewer machines stood (189 -> 172,
and 124 with a warm start and a 4-hour warm-up: many of logistic's machines run once every hour or three, the arqad
hive once in 2.8 hours, which a 2-hour warm-up cannot fill). The science machine then made packs, 33 of them, and
stood with a full output: **the in-game check's own sink was shut.** The mod marked every edge sink within 3.5 tiles
of an overflow port as an overflow sink (opened only for surplus), and the science port lay a tile from the saps and
moss overflows. Each overflow (and mixed) port now marks only the sink nearest it.

Py science 2 now lays out valid too (361 x 416): its corridor errors were the same 0/s links, and the three

## Afternoon: the governor, overflows per belt, and feeding what stands short

**Why `--governor` looked worse.** Mostly counting: "machines finished nothing while measured" counted machines planned
to craft less than once in the 20 measured minutes (134 of the 183 in the governor's run; a Py arqad hive crafts once
in 2.8 hours). Counted against the plan, the plain warm start had 35 machines idle that should have crafted, the
governor (every machine held to 1.5 times its plan) 49. The plain run's numbers were inflated too: its bones/bonemeal
loop ran at 9 times its plan on bones the check kept topping up as a seed. What the governor really did wrong:

- Holding every machine also held the loops that must run ahead of their plan to fill up (bones and bonemeal, barrels),
  and they jammed.
- Holding a machine whenever *some* machine was short of one of its ingredients (the next try, 52 idle) was too coarse:
  one item runs on many separate belts. A crude-oil barrel filler was held all the time for a tall-oil filler on
  another belt, and its own users starved.

Now a machine ahead of its plan waits only while a machine fed from the *same belt network* stands short of one of its
ingredients (belt networks are worked out once: belts joined through neighbours, undergrounds and splitters, and the
networks each machine's inserters take from and drop onto).

**Overflows.** Two faults kept supply from where it was needed:

- The split gave no overflow to a block that receives an item and also makes it. Logistic's gravel came from three
  blocks' nickel and iron crushers into one block that makes gravel too; its one user stood (no cellulose), the gravel
  had nowhere to go, and the crushers stopped with full outputs. Every receiving block now gets an overflow after its
  users, and the router ends the chain from the incoming link at it.
- The check opened an overflow sink when any maker of the item anywhere stood full. In Py science 2 one coke oven
  blocked on one belt let the overflow after the fluidized-coke block take 2 coke/s, while steel and graphite on other
  belts had none (planned use of coke: 0.68/s in all). A sink now opens only when a maker dropping onto its own belt
  network stands full.

**`--feed-short`** (the user's idea): every machine standing short of an ingredient gets it, and what was fed is counted
per item. With supply never short, what still stands is the layout's own fault; what had to be fed is where the
factory's own supply falls short, without the cascade behind it.
