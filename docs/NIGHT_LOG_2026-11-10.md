What the production runs showed on the way:

- **A walled-in port.** With ends in blocks the first production stitch failed in 2 minutes, every floorplan alike:
  "molten-glass cannot reach its port". Block 22's molten-glass input sat between molten-silver's void (valve, four
  pipes, sinkhole) and molten-solder's sinkhole; its corridor pipe had no tile to start on and no pipe-to-ground
  could come up anywhere clean. Ends now keep off each corridor port's way out.
- **Too strict at first.** The first version of that rule counted a chest's inserter beside a pipe port's way out,
  and resume dropped 17 of 24 saved blocks. Only what a pipe joins (pipes, valves, tanks, machines) matters beside it.
- **Lane pairs.** The block search now and then put a port right beside a lane pair's port, which resume drops; a
  resumed test lost a block that way. The trial now fails such a layout, and the router prices those tiles high.
- **Groups that never finish.** Routed in groups side by side, each group (about 160 links over 1000 x 900 tiles)
  spent its whole 33 minutes in negotiation and endgame, leaving the second pass no time. Each guided search now
  stays within three cells of its coarse route (the whole factory only where nothing fits there), groups get a
  third of the time, and a search heading for a few tiles no longer runs a distance transform over a million tiles.
