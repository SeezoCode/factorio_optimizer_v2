# Night log 2026-11-13: can a Py factory hold itself?

The question for the night: is there a plan and a set of rules under which a Py factory keeps making its science
with every rate a few percent off and after an outage, tested in the plan-level model, and what does the game say?
The answer and the evidence: [GO_NO_GO.md](GO_NO_GO.md). This log is the order things were found in.

## In the model

- **Rates with a margin** (`tools/plan-sim/margin.py`, solved by the planner's solver through `LpFileTest`): every
  item still in surplus with makers slow and users fast by a tolerance. Feasible for every plan up to 3%; the cost
  compounds round the loops (production science: 1.25, 1.62, 2.15 times the raw input at 1, 2, 3%).
- **`sim2.py`**: who gets a short item is decided item by item (fixed order, smallest user first, in proportion).
  With margins and smallest first, rates 5% off pass in 10 of 10 runs for seven of eight plans (Py science 4: 8).
  Without margins nothing passes.
- **Carriers** (barrels, cages, bricks) cannot be in surplus. Found by repeating the first stage of the program
  until nothing else falls short; balanced exactly, never overflowed, started low so a ring cannot lock.
- Two mistakes of mine on the way: a 0/s priming port counted as a free supply (the program switched a catalyst's
  own maker off), and the first sharing step lost material (the plain plan failed even held to plan).
- **Whole crafts**: the plain plan fails even with exact meters (68 to 82% and falling); a margin of 0.5% holds.
  Starting from empty takes hours under meters (chemical 29, Py science 2 83, Py science 4 75) until each meter
  starts with its users' first batch in hand; a flat allowance, a boost while stock is low, faster meters for the
  first hours and meters on only some machines all fail.
- **Late deliveries** need two hours' stock of each loop's starter items; with a minute's, the factory dies when
  priming ends.
- **A less entangled plan**: with by-product surplus at 3% of its price the planner still closes one loop of 515 of
  690 recipes and takes 43% more raw input.

## In game

New in the check: `--meter-file` and `--meter-start` (meters at a margin plan's rates, with the start-up allowance),
`--belt-pull` (output inserters work only for a belt with a user below its stock), `--virtual-bots` (items moved from
makers' outputs to machines in need). `--void-full` leaves carriers alone and deletes half rounded up.

No run on the existing layouts made a science pack in up to 14 game-hours. On belts the roots are makers at their
metered rates with unserved users: a metered machine dropping onto several belts gives each a share by turns, and
the first users on a belt stock up before the last sees anything. With `--virtual-bots` chemical science had 374 of
481 recipes at their rate after 12 hours (215 on belts after 14) and its science machine 8 of 10 ingredients in full;
production science at half rates had 14 machines that never crafted after 9 hours (130 on belts). Tables in the
verdict document.

Runs are in `solves/gm_*` (symlinks to the layouts, each with its `verify.out` and `verify_report.json`). The margin
plans' rate files are not kept: `tools/plan-sim/margin.sh <plan> <target> <prefix> 0.02 keep=1` writes one in under a
minute.
