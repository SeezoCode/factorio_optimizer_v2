# Night log 2026-11-14: from the verdict to a built factory

Follows [GO_NO_GO.md](GO_NO_GO.md). The 32-hour proof run left over from the morning made no science pack, so the
night starts there.

## The 32-hour proof run: alive, then dead once priming ended

Chemical science with robot-like transport, 28 game-hours primed and 4 measured: no pack, 23 of 512 machines idle
while measured. Its chains had run (526 plastic crafts, 3,264 fish renders) but at about half their rates, and in the
last four hours almost stopped: the fertilizer and seaweed loop, each short of the other, made 30 and 51 crafts where
228 and 428 were due. The emulation had no stock anywhere but the machines' own outputs, a few crafts each, which is
the case the model had named: a minute's stock dies within two hours of priming ending, two hours' stock carries on.

`verify.py --vb-buffer S`: what a machine stands full of goes to a storage holding S seconds of each item's use, from
which machines in need are served first; only what is past that is deleted. The report gets an hourly `timeline` of
crafts by recipe.

## Margins in the planner

`planner.margin` (0: off): after the exact balance the chosen recipes are solved once more so that every item made
and used inside is in surplus with makers slow and users fast by that share, no recipe below its exact rate. Carriers
that only go round are found by repeating a least-shortfall program until nothing else falls short, and balanced
exactly; plan.json records `margin` and `rings`. The surplus leaves as by-products. `MarginTest` checks the surplus on
vanilla chemical science. Py science 1 at 1%: 1.08 times the exact plan's machine time; logistic science: 1.12, 8
carriers. The planner's logistic plan holds in the model (whole crafts, five minutes' delay, first come first served,
rates 1% off): first science at hour 9, then 99 to 115% an hour for the 39 hours followed.

## Logistic science built for robots

No new router code: blocks of at most two machines (`blocks.max_machines` 2 by machines) and every item link between
blocks by robots (`blocks.bot_links` 1.0). One change was needed: robot links of one item now share one chest at
each end of a block (a port per link gave a maker for nine blocks nine belts to nine chests, and the split added
machines "so that its 9 belts can be fed").

- 337 machines in 169 blocks, all 412 item links by robots, 241 small by-products in chests beside their blocks.
- Every block had a layout 16 s after the split; the first stitch routed at **537 x 538** (the belt factory of
  2026-11-09 was 319 x 342). 21,591 entities, 84 roboports over 619 chests.

## The built factory stood still: turned blocks had lost their requests

First check of the built factory (real robots, meters at the plan's own rates, 7 game-hours): no pack, one or two of
337 machines working at any moment, 227 that never crafted. The report's robot lines gave it away: 1,680 robots, 1,677
idle, 59,524 items waiting in 239 provider chests, 341 of 380 requester chests empty, and the empty ones asked for
nothing. `turnLayout` (blocks/Rotate.kt) rebuilt every entity of a turned block field by field and left out the
fields added since: a requester's request, a chest's bar, a belt read by a loop gate. 260 of the 380 requesters sat
in turned blocks. It now copies the entity (`RotateTest` holds a requester, a barred chest and a read belt through a
turn). A turned block's loop gates lost their belt reading the same way.

Restitched from the saved blocks: 531 x 544, 21,221 entities, 90 roboports, all 380 requesters asking.

## What paced chemical science: one fluid, first come first served

The first packs of the night (chemical science, robot-like transport with a stock: 12 crafts by hour 10, half the
planned rate in the last two hours) did not last. The same set-up run on made 10 crafts by hour 8.4 and 12 by hour
17. Traced from the science machine: mechanical parts, shaft casting, antimony oxide, eleven levels down to
`ag-biomass-extraction` standing without steam, its steam maker (`depressurize-steam`) at a third of its rate, the
heat exchanger before that at a fifth, short of pressured water.

Pressured water is made at its metered rate (138 crafts of 500 in ten hours, 69,000). Three machines use it: a fusion
reactor that takes 10,000 a craft and crafts every two and a half hours (83% of the flow by plan), the heat exchanger
(100 a craft, 15%) and an egg incubator. The reactor stands next to the maker and holds 20,000 in its input box, two
crafts' worth, which is four hours of everything that is made. It took 66,000 of the 69,000 (57,000 by plan), the heat
exchanger 2,200 (10,400 by plan). Every other steam user sat on a full box.

So the game's machines each keep two crafts' worth of every input for themselves, a pipe gives to whoever is first,
and the pipes themselves hold next to nothing against a batch of 10,000. The emulation had a stock for items
(`--vb-buffer`) and none for fluids.

## The model, made to behave like the game's machines

`sim2.py` got the three things the game does and the model did not:

- `tank=2`: every machine keeps its own stock of each input, two crafts' worth, filled before anything else, first
  come first served in a fixed order, whether it works or not.
- `fluidk=F`: a fluid's pool is no more than its makers' output boxes (F batches of its biggest user on top): pipes
  without storage.
- `ringroom=F`: room in the carriers' pools for the whole ring (the game check gives carriers unlimited storage).
