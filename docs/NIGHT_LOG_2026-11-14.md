# Night log 2026-11-14: from the verdict to a built factory

Follows [GO_NO_GO.md](GO_NO_GO.md). The 32-hour proof run left over from the morning made no science pack, so the
night starts there.

## The 32-hour proof run: alive, then dead once priming ended

Chemical science with robot-like transport, 28 game-hours primed and 4 measured: no pack, 23 of 512 machines idle
while measured. Its chains had run (526 plastic crafts, 3,264 fish renders) but at about half their rates, and in the
last four hours almost stopped: the fertilizer and seaweed loop, each short of the other, made 30 and 51 crafts where
228 and 428 were due. The emulation had no stock anywhere but the machines' own outputs, a few crafts each, which is
the case the model had named: a minute's stock dies within two hours of priming ending, two hours' stock carries on.

`verify.py --vb-buffer S`: what a machine stands full of goes to a storage holding S seconds of each item's use, from
which machines in need are served first; only what is past that is deleted. The report gets an hourly `timeline` of
crafts by recipe.

## Margins in the planner

`planner.margin` (0: off): after the exact balance the chosen recipes are solved once more so that every item made
and used inside is in surplus with makers slow and users fast by that share, no recipe below its exact rate. Carriers
that only go round are found by repeating a least-shortfall program until nothing else falls short, and balanced
exactly; plan.json records `margin` and `rings`. The surplus leaves as by-products. `MarginTest` checks the surplus on
vanilla chemical science. Py science 1 at 1%: 1.08 times the exact plan's machine time; logistic science: 1.12, 8
carriers. The planner's logistic plan holds in the model (whole crafts, five minutes' delay, first come first served,
rates 1% off): first science at hour 9, then 99 to 115% an hour for the 39 hours followed.

## Logistic science built for robots

No new router code: blocks of at most two machines (`blocks.max_machines` 2 by machines) and every item link between
blocks by robots (`blocks.bot_links` 1.0). One change was needed: robot links of one item now share one chest at
each end of a block (a port per link gave a maker for nine blocks nine belts to nine chests, and the split added
machines "so that its 9 belts can be fed").

- 337 machines in 169 blocks, all 412 item links by robots, 241 small by-products in chests beside their blocks.
- Every block had a layout 16 s after the split; the first stitch routed at **537 x 538** (the belt factory of
  2026-11-09 was 319 x 342). 21,591 entities, 84 roboports over 619 chests.

## What paced chemical science: one fluid, first come first served

The first packs of the night (chemical science, robot-like transport with a stock: 12 crafts by hour 10, half the
planned rate in the last two hours) did not last. The same set-up run on made 10 crafts by hour 8.4 and 12 by hour
17. Traced from the science machine: mechanical parts, shaft casting, antimony oxide, eleven levels down to
`ag-biomass-extraction` standing without steam, its steam maker (`depressurize-steam`) at a third of its rate, the
heat exchanger before that at a fifth, short of pressured water.

Pressured water is made at its metered rate (138 crafts of 500 in ten hours, 69,000). Three machines use it: a fusion
reactor that takes 10,000 a craft and crafts every two and a half hours (83% of the flow by plan), the heat exchanger
(100 a craft, 15%) and an egg incubator. The reactor stands next to the maker and holds 20,000 in its input box, two
crafts' worth, which is four hours of everything that is made. It took 66,000 of the 69,000 (57,000 by plan), the heat
exchanger 2,200 (10,400 by plan). Every other steam user sat on a full box.

So the game's machines each keep two crafts' worth of every input for themselves, a pipe gives to whoever is first,
and the pipes themselves hold next to nothing against a batch of 10,000. The emulation had a stock for items
(`--vb-buffer`) and none for fluids.

## The model, made to behave like the game's machines

`sim2.py` got the three things the game does and the model did not:

- `tank=2`: every machine keeps its own stock of each input, two crafts' worth, filled before anything else, first
  come first served in a fixed order, whether it works or not.
- `fluidk=F`: a fluid's pool is no more than its makers' output boxes (F batches of its biggest user on top): pipes
  without storage.
- `ringroom=F`: room in the carriers' pools for the whole ring (the game check gives carriers unlimited storage).

My first version of `tank` capped a machine at two crafts a step. That throttled the fast machines (iron plate at 89%
of its rate) and took four more plans down, which for twenty minutes looked like a finding. Fixed (the stock also
covers what the machine crafts in a step) and rerun. Margins of 1%, cold start, 48 hours primed, 16 left alone,
two random cases each, target made in those 16 hours:

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Py science 1 | 56 | 101% | 101% | 101% | 101% |
| logistic | 325 | 101% | 101% | 101% | 101% |
| Py science 2 | 406 | 102% | 52% and 84% | 102% | 102% |
| chemical | 512 | 96% and 101% | 0% and 62% | 96% and 101% | 0% both |
| Py science 3 | 681 | 101% | 96% and 101% | 101% | 101% |
| production | 869 | 101% | 101% | 101% | 101% |
| Py science 4 | 1,102 | 101% | 101% | 101% | 101% |
| utility | 2,524 | 101% | 101% | 101% | 101% |

What it says:

- **Hoarding and first come first served do not break the design.** With a stock of every item and fluid and room
  for the carriers all eight plans hold, as they did when the smallest user was served first.
- **Fluids need a stock too.** Kept in nothing but their makers' output boxes, chemical science and Py science 2
  lose most of their target. One batch of the fluid's biggest user in store is enough. Serving the smallest user
  first is also enough (the laid-out chemical plan, four cases: 0 to 55% first come first served, 102% in all four
  smallest first, 102% in all four with a batch in store). Letting a starved machine catch up later (a deeper meter
  bucket, `bsecs`) is not: 23 to 69%.
- **A ring must have room for all its carriers.** Without it chemical science stops dead: cages pile up until the
  renderers stand full. The count of carriers put into a ring has to be fixed, or its storage big enough to hold
  them all.
- **Starting takes far longer when machines hoard.** The planner's own logistic plan (337 machines, the one built
  for robots) makes its first pack at hour 9 from empty when items go straight to whoever crafts next, and at hour
  36 when every machine first fills two crafts' worth of each input. Start-up allowances (for every user, doubled)
  do not shorten it; a deeper bucket lengthens it (hour 43). Meters three times faster for the first 16 hours bring
  it to hour 13, and the plan settles at 99% once they are back to normal (`fill=3:16`; `--meter-fill 3:16` in the
  check). Twelve-hour checks of this factory could not have shown a pack.

The first pack from empty, by plan (1% margins, first come first served, carriers with room; hour of the first
pack; every plan then holds 96 to 102% over the last 16 of 64 hours):

| Science | No hoarding | Two crafts hoarded | Hoarded, meters 3 times faster for 16 hours | Hoarded, but need first |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Py science 1 | 2 | 18 | 5 | 2 |
| logistic | 6 | 25 | 17 | 8 |
| Py science 2 | 9 | 21 | 22 | 13 |
| chemical | 3 | 49 | 23 | 7 |
| Py science 3 | 2 | 12 | 27 | 9 |
| production | 3 | 13 | 29 | 4 |
| Py science 4 | 8 | 32 | 49 | 22 |

- **Hoarding turns a start of hours into one of a day or two.** The fast start helps three plans, changes nothing
  for one and sets three back.
- **Need first is the start-up rule that works for all seven** (`needfirst=1`): of the machines waiting for an item,
  those holding less than one craft's worth are filled to one craft before anybody stocks a second. First pack at
  hour 2 to 22, and every plan holds 101 to 102% afterwards. Keeping only one craft's worth per machine (`tank=1`)
  does as well for four plans and worse for production (hour 6), logistic (13) and chemical (32).
- In a blueprint need first is a wire: a requester chest whose requests are set from its machine's missing
  ingredients, or an inserter that works only while its machine is short. `verify.py --request-need` does it to the
  built factory's requester chests (379 of 380 found their machines): they ask for one craft's worth (two minutes'
  flow where the machine is fast), and only while their machine holds less.
- In game the fluid boxes hold up to four crafts' worth, not two (chemical science's steam users: 400 where a craft
  takes 100), and a requester chest as built adds ten minutes' worth or ten items (in the model that costs logistic
  science another one to six hours).
- For the built logistic plan itself (337 machines, five minutes' delivery): first pack at hour 36 hoarded, 22 need
  first, 13 with the fast start alone, **9 with need first and meters three times faster for the first ten hours**,
  99 to 115% an hour from then on.

Two things in this run are the check's doing, and one I could not account for:

- **The check threw away tin plate its users were waiting for.** Tin plate comes 63 to a craft, and the plan makes
  more than it uses (about 60% leaves as a by-product). `--void-full` halves a machine's output every second it
  stands full, and so deleted 85 of the 126 plates of the two crafts. The four users took 20 and hold none; one
  circuit takes 20 through its solder, capacitors and resistors. A real overflow exit would keep the plates on its
  belt, not destroy them, so this is a fault of the check and not of the layout, and it most likely cost this run

  cottongut cubs).

  one. They are not in a chest, on a belt or among the voided items in the report. The maker has a second exit, an

So the second run does not show a pack and does not show that none would come: both faults sit on the one chain the
pack waits for. It does show that the start from empty is the wrong test for a factory like this. Sixty game-hours
are sixty hours at a player's desk, and the loop still had not closed.

## What the three long runs say together

- **The cheap proof holds as far as it went.** Chemical science with its items and fluids carried through a stock
  made packs at the planned rate for the last two of 48 hours, once the one big fluid hoard had filled.
- **The built proof is still open, neither way.** No pack in 60 hours in either run. Nothing in them points at the
  layout or the robots; everything points at the start: a loop of about 25 recipes through tin, fish and circuits
  that has to go round once from empty, at rates of a craft in hours.
- **Start-up time goes with the hoards, counted in crafts, over the craft rate.** A machine planned at a craft in
  three hours that stocks two crafts of an input passes nothing on for six hours, and twenty such steps in a row are
  days. The plan's size does not matter to this, its slowest steps do: halving the target rate doubles the wait.
- **A factory like this should not be started empty.** What the check does in its warm-up, a builder has to do once:
  every loop's starter items in stock, enough to last until the loop has gone round (rule 4, sized by the loop's
  first round and not by an hour's use), or every machine handed its first two crafts.
- **The check needs three repairs before the next long run**: keep feeding starters until each loop has made its
  own (and say when that was), let `--void-full` leave alone what a user still lacks, and find the reduced tin.
- **The model is too early by more than two times** on the built factory (hour 36 against more than 60). Its
  other claim, that hoarding costs the start and not the steady rate, held in the chemical run.

## What was built

- Planner: `planner.margin`, the margin stage (`MarginTest`). Robot links of one item share one chest at each end
  of a block. Turned blocks keep requests, bars and belt readings (`RotateTest`).
- Check (`verify.py`, `control.lua`): `--vb-buffer`, `--vb-fluids`, `--vb-share small`, `--request-need`,

  many requesters were mapped to machines.
- Model (`sim2.py`): `tank`, `fluidk`, `ringroom`, `needfirst`, `reqmin`, `prog0=users`, `prog0f`, `trace=3`.

## Wrong turns of the night

- The model's first `tank` throttled fast machines; for twenty minutes four plans looked broken by hoarding.
- The fluid stock first ignored temperatures, then turned out not to see fluids that leave the output box within a
  tick, and the fix for that (small users drawing from bigger users' boxes) made no pack in 14 hours.
- A start allowance that counts the users' own allowances (`--meter-start-depth`) grows without bound round Py's
  loops: at depth 2 a tenth of chemical science's recipes would start with more than 150 crafts in hand.
