# Night log 2026-11-15: a warm start, two layout flaws and what the check itself was doing wrong

Follows [NIGHT_LOG_2026-11-14.md](NIGHT_LOG_2026-11-14.md) and the verdict in [GO_NO_GO.md](GO_NO_GO.md). The plan
for the night: repair the check's three known faults, run the built Py logistic science factory (337 machines, real
robots) from a warm start, and decide on that. Target: 6.1 science packs a game-hour.

## The three known faults of the check, repaired

- Loop starters were handed out only during the warm-up. `--seed-through` goes on while measuring, and the report
  counts them by hour (`seeded` in the timeline), so a loop that only runs on what it is handed shows.
- The sink for full machines halved a "full" machine's output every second. A craft of 63 tin plates reads full until
  its inserters have carried it off: 85 of 126 plates were deleted in the 60-hour run. It now waits (`--void-wait S`,
  30) and leaves for ten minutes more what a user still lacks (`void_kept`).
- The report says where everything is (`where`: belts, chests by kind, hands, robots, machines' inputs and outputs).

A warm start is `--prime-all`: every machine begins with two crafts' worth of its ingredients.

## Layout flaw 1: the overflow exit takes from the first item

A block built for robots gives a maker two unfiltered output inserters, one onto a belt to its provider chest and one
onto a belt to the block's overflow chest. They take turns from the first item made. The overflow chest's own inserter
stays shut until the belt backs up, so the belt before it fills first: 3 to 8 tiles, 8 items a tile. Py logistic
science has 196 such exits, 775 belt tiles, room for about 6,200 items. This is where nine of the fourteen reduced tin
of the 60-hour run went.

Rule 10: **an overflow exit takes only once the stock is filled.**

- Check: `--overflow-last` shuts such an exit until its machine has stood full for a minute.
- An existing test blueprint: `tools/factorio-verify/overflow_last.py` writes a logistic condition ("more than N
  stacks in the network") onto the 196 inserters.
- Planner: `blocks.overflow_after_stacks` (2), `route.addOverflowLast`, `OverflowLastTest`.

A correction found later in the night: the rule must leave alone a by-product whose only way out is its overflow
chest. The quartz crusher has an exit for crushed quartz to a user, one to a provider chest, and one for its stone to
an overflow chest alone. Shut, the stone stopped the crusher for a minute at every opening in the check; with the
condition in the blueprint it would never open, which is the likeliest reason the blueprint version below did worse
than the emulation. Both now gate an exit only where another exit carries the same item.

## Meters as a budget, not a speed limit

The check's meter was a bucket of one craft plus a minute of the planned rate. Whatever a machine could not craft
while it waited was lost for good: at hour 12.6 the lab instrument machine had made 33 of 91 planned crafts and stood

## Fault 4 of the check: hot steam handed to users of cool steam

Steam is made at 1,000 degrees and cooled in two steps, each step making 1.8 times as much. The run without starters

coolers' boxes were the biggest. In a built factory each temperature has its own pipes. Now only at the temperature
the stock would give it.

## Fault 5: a warm start that left big boxes empty

The plan holds a wax barreller, a craft in three hours (the solver's way of losing 0.005 wax a second). Its input box
holds 1,000 wax. Of the first 1,200 wax made it had 862, the mold machine on the same pipe stood at 18 of the 20 it
takes for eight hours, and casting, iron sticks, rich dust, chromium and sulfuric acid behind it. `--prime-full` fills
every fluid input box to the top, as in a factory that has run for a while (furnace-type machines too, which have no
recipe before they are fed).

This one is not only the check's: a real factory fills that box first as well. See "What the planner still owes".

## Fault 6: the test copy's overflow deleted half the boric acid

The science pack's chain runs boric acid, boron trioxide, lens, lab instrument, plasmids, animal sample. In every run
of both nights boron trioxide stood at 53 to 57% of its plan with its maker at 100% or more, and the chain behind it
at 30 to 40%.

A fluid overflow in a test copy is an infinity pipe that keeps its pipe "at most" so full. It deletes, at once and
entirely, whatever is in the pipe at another temperature than its filter's. The planner wrote no temperature, so the
filter stood at the fluid's default: 0 for Py's boric acid, which is made at 10. Released for one craft with the
user's box nearly full, the maker's 500 put 148 into the box and the rest was gone five ticks later.

Of 133 overflows in this factory it is the only one, and it sits on the science pack's chain: the run with starters
made 3.3 packs an hour from hour 12 to 19, 55% of plan, which is this cap.

- Check: at the start a filter at a temperature no machine here makes the fluid at takes the one they do
  (`sink_temperatures` in the report).
- Planner: `GameConstants.MADE_TEMPERATURES`, written into a test copy's infinity pipes (`TemperaturesTest`).

## Layout flaw 2: the provider chest takes half, whoever needs it

The same shape as flaw 1. A maker with a user on its own belt and a provider chest for users elsewhere has two
inserters that take turns. The limestone maker's provider chest held 625 limestone after eight hours and the network
2,363 after twenty, robots asking for none of it, while the sodium alginate machine on the maker's belt ran at 78% of
its plan for want of limestone. Lead plate and the lead-antimony alloy: 80%.

Rule 11: **a provider exit works only while the stock is below its level; the user beside the maker comes first.**
With rule 10 at the same level one of the two is always open.

- Check: `--provider-level STACKS` (61 exits in this factory).
- Planner: `blocks.provider_until_stacks` (off unless set), `route.addProviderLevel`, tested in `OverflowLastTest`.

Found later: the provider chests of this factory had lost their slot limit, which would have done most of this by
itself (see "A fault of the stitch").

## A fault of the stitch: provider chests without their limit

Looking for a way to try rules 10 and 11 as the planner writes them, not as the check stands in for them: a provider
chest is meant to fill two slots only (`blocks.bot_provider_slots`), and none of the built factory's 239 has a limit.
A block's layout saved for `--resume` did not keep it, and this factory was stitched from saved blocks. So the coke
chest holds 4,800 and the limestone chest took half of what its maker made for twenty hours: flaw 2 is in good part
this. A chest with its limit stops taking at two stacks and the neighbour gets the rest.

It also showed that rule 10 as first written could not work beside the limit: "more than two stacks in the network"
is never true of an item with one provider chest of two slots. Repaired together:

- the limit is saved with a block's layout, and put back on provider chests that came without it
  (`route.withProviderBars`);
- the levels of rules 10 and 11 stay half a stack under what one provider chest may hold;
- a factory that cannot be written says so (the first re-stitch tonight ended "valid" and wrote nothing; why is
  below).

The factory stitched again from its saved blocks with all of this (`n7_built`: 239 chests with their limit, 195
overflow exits and 29 provider exits with their condition in the blueprint, valid) and run with no stand-in for the
two rules: **6 packs in the first hour and a half and none in the next three.** Limestone's users elsewhere had made a
twentieth of their plan. 17 of the 224 inserters with a logistic condition stand outside every roboport's logistic
area, where such a condition never holds, and one of them is the limestone maker's only way to its provider chest.
The roboports had been placed over the chests alone. Repaired: they go over these inserters too, and one that is
still out of reach keeps no condition. The same must have held the one earlier blueprint run of rule 10
(`n3_real_e`), which was read as the by-product case alone.

The stitch with that repair then came out valid and could not be written, as the very first one had, in silence:
turning a block left a local fluid sink's valve tile where it was, and with another pipe on that tile by chance the
writer threw. The tile turns with its port now (`RotateTest`). Stitched once more (`n9_built`): written, 239 chests
with their limit, 224 conditions, none out of reach (the failed one: 17, by the same count). It has not run in game.

## The start-up allowance does harm in a warm factory

`--meter-start` gives every meter enough crafts in hand to serve each user of its products once (`n4`: twice). From
empty that is what gets a slow maker's users going. In a factory that already holds two crafts of everything it lets
small recipes run far ahead of their plan on shared makers: in 4.1 hours the quartz powder machine took 420 crushed
quartz where its plan has 50, and melamine 1,060 ammonia where its plan has 12. Packs by hour 8: 24 with the
allowance, 35 without. An allowance belongs to a start from empty and should run out as the first crafts are made.

## First fills

What a warm start of two crafts does not hold, and a slow item then pays for once:

- **Fluid boxes**: fault 5, the wax barreller's 1,000.
- **A local user's belt and input.** A machine fed by a belt from its neighbour takes turns with the provider chest
  until its belt (8 items a tile) and its input are full. Rayon is made five at a time every 40 minutes; of the first
  35, thirteen went to the belt and the input of the small battery recipe beside the maker, which uses two in three
  hours. In `n5_base` this is the hours 3.2 to 4.8 with two packs.
- **The first machine on a shared requester belt** takes what passes until it holds more than it will use for hours
  (6 molds where it needs 1, 30 crushed quartz where it needs 5) before the next one sees any.

None of these recur once filled. They are why a factory's first hours say little, warm start or not.

## What the planner still owes

- **Rules 10 and 11 in the blueprint, run in game.** Both are in the planner (`blocks.overflow_after_stacks`,
  `blocks.provider_until_stacks`) and tested, but every rate above comes from the check's stand-in on the factory
  built before them. The one factory stitched with them failed on roboport reach within three hours; the repair is
  in and not yet run. This is the first thing to do next.
- **Need first that can be built** (rule 9): a requester chest whose request follows what its machines lack needs a
  wire from the machines; the check only stands in for it. Fast links need two minutes of flow at hand (fault 7).
- **Meters as a budget** (a day's catch-up) rather than a bucket of a minute, and no start-up allowance once the
  factory is warm.
- **No trickle recipes with big boxes**: the wax barreller is a craft in three hours that the solver uses to lose
  0.005 wax a second; its box of 1,000 is filled first. Either such recipes are pruned for an overflow, or their first
  fill is counted into the starter stock.
- **A sink's temperature** is done (`MADE_TEMPERATURES`); a fluid made at two temperatures into one overflow is not.
- **Starting**: nothing tonight was started from empty. The factory as a player would build it needs its first fills
  handed over or days of game time (the second night's finding stands).
- **Power**: the 197 blocks are 197 power networks; the stitch leaves joining them to the player.

## Verdict

The mark set before the night was 90% of the pack rate from a warm start, or decay.

- **It does not decay and nothing deadlocks.** The built factory, warm, makes packs every hour for 16 to 20 hours,
  closes every loop without starters, and has 312 to 320 of 336 recipes at their rate.
- **With the last fault of the check repaired it holds the plan**: 51 packs in the measured eight hours where 48.5
  are due, 96 of 97 over all 16, nothing handed to it, 324 of 336 recipes at their rate. Before that repair: 68%
  without starters, 85% with.
- **Every shortfall traced tonight ended in one named cause, and none was a ring that cannot turn.** Seven were the
  check's, two a maker's exits, two the stitch's, one the roboports'.
- **Not shown**: the planner's own blueprint holding that rate. The 105% is the factory built two nights ago with
  the check standing in for rules 9, 10 and 11 and for a fluid stock. The first factory stitched with rules 10 and 11
  in it failed for reasons now repaired; the repaired one has not run. Nor a start from empty, nor more than a day.
  (Run since: "The day after" below. 97% of the plan in the measured half, and one named fault left in it.)

So: go on. The mark is passed with stand-ins, and the next step is one run, not a programme: `solves/n9_built`,
warm, 16 hours, no stand-in for rules 10 and 11. If that holds what the stand-in held, the built proof asked for two
nights ago is there in steady state, and what is left is building rule 9 and a fluid stock, and the start. If it does
not, the trace of it is a day's work of the kind done several times tonight.

### Rule 10b: an overflow exit for an item no provider holds

Rule 10 guards an exit by the robot network's stock. An item its maker hands straight to the machine beside it is in
no network, and the hand-over and the overflow exit share what is made (yotoi leaves: 65 of 170 on the belt to their
overflow chest, bedding at 65%). Such an exit now gets a circuit condition, the item above a craft's worth less one,
and a red wire to the using machine, which puts what it holds on the wire without the craft in hand (`d3e024b`).
Found by trying in game: a machine's blueprint entry is `read_contents` with `include_in_crafting` off (it is on by
default), and a wire reaches 9 tiles between the entities' boxes, not their centres. The rule measures between
centres all the same, as the validator does: the first stitch with it measured between boxes, and three floorplans
failed on four wires of 9.8 to 12 tiles that the game would have taken.

In the factory stitched with it (`n13_built`, 544 x 525, valid at the first size): 11 exits wired, none out of
reach; in game 11 machines read their contents and each of the 11 inserters sees its item on its wire. Thirty more
open exits carry an item no provider holds and have no hand-over beside them: by-products nothing in the plan uses
(tin rejects, raw coal, photophores), the pack itself, and items whose one user sits on a belt of the maker's own
block (agar, coarse, grade 1 tin, cottongut pups, brains). The last kind is not guarded by anything yet. With the
check's stand-in closing them all the pack read the same in the big factory, where coke was the limit; it is not the
same once coke is cured (below).
