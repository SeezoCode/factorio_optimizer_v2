# Night log, 2026-11-16: belts with by-products

The question for the night: can a belt-built Py factory be made to work, with overflow control at the producer and
circuit logic where it helps? The way asked for: give the factory all the help the check can give, then take the help
away piece by piece.

The factory: Py logistic science as laid out for belts on 2026-11-09 (`solves/py_logistic_science`: 325 machines in
12 blocks, 319 x 342, plain plan without a margin). The test is the one the robot-built factory passed yesterday: a
warm start, 16 game-hours, the second half measured, 6.06 pack crafts an hour planned and 48.5 due in the measured
half. Every run is this one layout; nothing was stitched or laid out tonight.

In short:

- Overflow at the producer is needed, can be decided by a circuit at the machine, and was built in the game from
  real parts (90 inserters, 156 decider combinators on this factory). Under the check's flow rules it did better
  than the check's own stand-in: 50 packs in the measured half against 40.
- It does not make the belt factory work. Alone it makes 3 packs in four hours. The belt factory makes packs only
  with two more rules of the check on top, pull and quotas. Then it makes 82 to 105% of the planned packs in the
  measured half, but it is fed loop carriers all the way and it is running down: recipes at their rate fall from
  about 305 to 246 to 259 of 313 over the 16 hours in the three finished runs that are not also fed starters to the
  end.
- What stops belts here is not by-products. It is that a belt gives its goods to whoever stands first, and that a
  maker with two belts gives each half.

## What the game does (tried on a test surface)

- **A machine stops taking ingredients once a few crafts' worth of a product lies in its output**: two to five by
  the recipe's speed (below), counted by the expected amount where a product comes with a chance. It reads "output
  full" from then on and still crafts what it already holds. Ingredients put in by script are not held back, so a
  scripted test runs on to a full stack and proves nothing.
- **A machine set to read its contents puts products and fluids on the wire too**, its output boxes with them. A
  decider combinator beside it can therefore tell which product stops it.
- **The relief circuit works as wired**: a decider with "tin at 5 or more AND dust under 5" on a wire from the
  machine, its output enabling a filter inserter out of the machine. With the dust leaving, the machine ran on and
  the inserter took tin whenever it reached 5. With the dust held back, the dust reached its limit, the inserter
  stood and the machine stopped.
- **In a decider, "and" binds tighter than "or"**: "A or B and C" with only A on gives 1.
- **Splitters have circuit control in this game version** (input side, output side and filter set by conditions).
  A splitter cannot shut an output: what stops a belt is a belt tile with an enable condition.
- **An infinity pipe with no filter is not a closed pipe end: it destroys what its pipes hold** (150 water in two
  pipes and such a pipe: gone in three seconds). And Py's pipes do not join the plain `pipe`.

- **As laid out the factory makes nothing to speak of**, with or without an overflow at every producer. Two hundred
  of its 309 recipes run at more than three times their plan and forty-four at under 85% of it: what is made is not
  what is wanted.
- **The flow rules alone stall on the first by-product nobody takes** (cages in the slaughterhouse's output).
  **With an overflow at the producer they make packs for 16 hours: 82% of plan in the measured half.** That is under
  the 90% mark and under the robot-built factory's 97%, and it is not steady: recipes at 90% of their rate went 306,
  296, 281, 246 of 313 over the four quarters of the run.
- **And the 82% is not all the factory's own.** The carrier rule gives a machine that has stood two minutes without
  its barrel or cage five minutes' worth, as often as it stands so. In the measured half it handed in 416 caged
  scrondrix, a third of what the slaughterhouse used, and 756 water barrels, a seventh of the plan's use, while the
  blunt overflow deleted 417 cages at the slaughterhouse. The cage loop did not close; it was fed.
- **Take either flow rule away and it fails within three hours.** Without quotas the makers of iron plate, copper
  plate and sodium hydroxide run at four to eight times their plan and the gear wheels, the methanol and the
  batteries get none: one user early on the belt takes it all. Without pull every machine with a way out runs on.
- **Meters on top make it worse** (142 recipes behind): a metered maker splits what it makes between its belts by
  turns, and the belt that needs it gets half.
- **Starters past the warm-up are a big help where given**: that run was fed 250,000 methanol and 190,000 sulfuric
  acid an hour to the end. Its 87% is not a factory running on its own; the 82% is the number.

## Overflow at the producer: what the rule has to be

The idea asked about: let a product out at the machine that makes it, so a full belt never stops a machine that is
wanted for something else. The check had a blunt form (`--void-full`: a machine that stood full two minutes has half
of every product deleted). Tonight's work was to state it as something a circuit at the machine can decide: **a
product at its limit in the machine's output is taken out while another product of that machine, one some machine
uses, is under its limit.** Four things had to be right before it did what the blunt form does, each found by
tracing one machine that ran away or stood still.

1. **Every way out but a target's has to be under the rule.** Leftover belts, planned surpluses, fluid overflows:
   each one left open made whatever went onto it look wanted for good. A lamp maker with methane and oxygen beside
   the lamps ran at 5,000 times its plan; a nexelit crusher with tailings dust on a leftovers belt made 14,700
   crafts in four hours.
2. **The limit is the game's, not a fixed number of crafts.** A machine stops taking ingredients at two crafts'
   worth of a product when its craft takes 1.25 s or longer, three at 0.75 to 1 s, five at 0.25 s (from every
   machine standing full in three runs; put in directly for one 2-second recipe), and at any count once the next
   craft would not fit the stack. With five for all, the slaughterhouse stood full of two pineal glands and was
   never relieved, and the animal chain behind it ran at 6% of plan. With the right limit it ran at 96%.
3. **Loop carriers have to be put in** (water barrels, crude oil barrels, cages), as a player does by hand and as
   the flow rules do themselves.
4. **It has to take from the machine itself, not through a port other makers share.** The cheap way to build it is
   a gate on the layout's own overflow port: no new belts. But one maker's relief then moves the whole belt and every
   other maker on it has room again. Built that way from real combinators it made 4 packs in 11.5 hours.

With those in, relief at the machine carries the factory through the warm-up as the blunt form does, by script and
from real parts. After that the runs part, and one stops:

| Under the flow rules, the overflow at the producer is | Packs by hour 8 (48.5 due) | After hour 8 |
|---|---:|---|
| the blunt form (`--void-full`) | 47 | 40 in the measured half (82%) |
| relief by script (`--relief`) | 46 | 14 in the measured half (29%): 5, 5 and 4 an hour, then none from hour 11 |
| relief from real parts, 90 inserters (`--relief-exits`) | 49 | 50 in the measured half (103%) |
| the same, first build: 14 of 91 inserters and 34 of 157 deciders stood without power | 44 | 51 in the measured half (105%) |

The run that stops does so three hours into the measured half, when the sap tree loop behind the rubber runs out of
fertilizer and muddy sludge. Those are two of the loop starters the check tops up during the warm-up only. Whether
relief let out more of that loop's goods than the blunt form did was not traced.

The two runs with real parts are the best belt runs of the night by packs, and the pack count flatters them.
Recipes at 90% of their rate went 303, 289, 271, 259 and 305, 292, 275, 248 of 313 over the four quarters, as in
the run with the blunt form; about twenty recipes made nothing in the measured half, the lead for the batteries (3
to 4% of plan) and the sap tree loop among them; and the check handed in 520 and 546 caged scrondrix, nearly half of
what the slaughterhouse used. The packs of the last hours came out of stock. No belt run of the night is steady at
hour 16.

**On its own it does not make a factory**, in any form tried:

| Relief at the machine, with loop carriers, and | Packs | Left behind |
|---|---|---|
| nothing else | 3 by hour 3.9 (24 due) | gear wheels 5% of plan beside an iron plate maker at 775% |
| a user it can reach standing short of the other product | 3 by hour 3.7 | the same |
| quotas, no pull | 4 by hour 5.6 | 89 recipes behind |
| pull, no quotas | 6 by hour 5.7 | gear wheels 2%, iron plate maker 777% |

The reason is in what it lets out. In the run with nothing else, 3.9 hours: 1.5 million steam, 1.3 million water,
0.7 million hydrogen, 95,000 yaedols spores. A product "still leaving" is no sign that anyone needs it: in a web
where every by-product is somebody's ingredient there is always room somewhere, each machine runs for its
neighbour's appetite, and the neighbour's other product goes out by the same rule. The sand users ran at 40 and 100
times their plan, the stick maker beside the gear wheels at five times its plan on the plates the gear wheels
lacked. Relief keeps a wanted machine from stopping. It cannot say which machine is wanted; that is pull, and who
gets a shared belt's goods is quotas.

## Rate caps in place of quotas

A cap on every machine needs no combinator at the machine (an enable condition against a clock on a shared wire), so
it was the hope for a cheap stand-in. With relief at the machine and loop carriers:

| Every machine held to | Packs at each 1.6-hour mark (9.7 due per mark) |
|---|---|
| 2 times its planned rate | 6, 6, 6, 21, 24, 24, 24 (stopped at hour 11) |
| 3 times | 6, 6, 24, 24, 24, 24, 24 (stopped at hour 11) |
| 5 times | 6, 24, 24, 24 (stopped at hour 6.5) |
| 10 times | 6, 16, 16, 16 (stopped at hour 6.5) |

Each stands at the start (a capped maker fills its belts slowly, and splits what it makes between them by turns),
makes a burst once the first belts are full, and then stops at the same 24 packs (16 under the loosest cap). There
the pack waits for animal samples, those for plasmids, those for lab instruments, and the lab instruments for rubber
(next section). At hour 8.4 under the 3-times cap fluidize-coke also had no copper plate beside a copper plate maker
at twice its plan: a cap bounds each user's appetite, it does not hand the plate to the one that needs it. As a duty
cycle against shared clocks, which is the form with no combinator at the machine, it did worse still in its one run
(96 recipes behind at hour 2.2, with the limit of correction 2 still wrong). So caps are not a stand-in for quotas
on this evidence.

## Why the best runs slow down: one maker, two belts

The runs that work lose their lab instruments as the hours go, and the capped runs stop at 24 packs, for one
cause, traced in three of them. The rubber maker puts its rubber onto two belts by turns: one to the lab instruments, which the
plan gives 95% of it (67 an hour), one to the stopper maker (4 an hour). In the run with flow rules and relief, by
hour 13: 1,240 rubber made, 640 used by lab instruments, 264 by stoppers, 353 lying on the stopper's belt and none
on the other. The lab instruments ran at 73% of plan, and plasmids, animal samples and the pack behind them.

The stitch knows such makers and notes them as harmless "until belts back up". For an item made 71 an hour onto a
29-tile belt that is most of a day. It is a fault of the layout, not of any flow rule, and none of tonight's rules
sees it. What would: the inserter toward a belt works only while that belt holds less than a few crafts' worth of
its users (a wire from the inserter to the belt it drops onto, which can read what the whole belt holds; no
combinator). That is untested. The check's nearest rule, `--belt-pull`, looks at the user and not at the belt (the
inserter works while a user on that belt holds less than its stock); started late with the flow rules and relief, it
had 286 rubber on the stopper's belt by hour 3.2. It does not do it.

## What a blueprint would need

Built in the game from real parts tonight, by script on the finished layout (no block was laid out again):

| Part | Built | Without room |
|---|---:|---:|
| Relief at the machine: a filter inserter into a chest of its own, filters set by the circuit | 90 of 91 machines | 1 |
| Deciders for it, one per product that can stop its machine, on a wire from the machine | 156 | 0 |
| Demand: a decider at each machine per ingredient, "less than a craft's worth, the craft in hand counted", on a green wire over all 235 poles | 775 | 0 |
| Pull: a decider at each maker that reads the demand it can serve and enables the machine | 317 (4 machines need none) | 0 |
| For comparison, relief through the layout's ports: deciders, a red wire over the poles, a gated belt at each port | 142 deciders, 112 gates, 39 extra poles | 1 gate out of reach |

With pull and relief both built, 8 of the 91 relief inserters found no powered tile beside their machine (the
deciders had taken them); those machines stayed with the script.

What the parts give, by tonight's runs:

- **Relief at the machine works from real parts** and is the right place for it. Its items need somewhere to go: a
  chest that robots empty, or a belt to a port.
- **Pull from real parts switches machines as meant** (278 of 325 off at the start, makers waking as their users run
  short, 8 packs in the first 1.6 hours) **and then stood at 8 packs**, behind the barrel loops. The check's carrier
  rule and its quotas look for machines that stand short while switched on; a machine switched off by its own circuit
  is invisible to them. The barrel loops were given 263 water barrels in four hours, where the best scripted run was
  given 3,579 over its sixteen. So this run is no verdict on built pull either way: it needs the carrier rule taught
  to see such machines, and one more run.
- **Quotas have no part.** A quota needs each machine's planned intake and what it has taken so far: a counter and a
  clock per machine and ingredient. Nothing cheaper was found (the caps above).

## Faults of mine in the test, each of which cost a batch of runs

- The first relief runs kept the fluid overflows open (nine tenths full, always taking): every fluid looked wanted.
- "Closed" fluid ports were infinity pipes with their filter cleared. Those destroy fluid. Five batches ran with
  every fluid port a hole; found when a propene maker had made 52,000 propene and its pipes were empty.
- The sealed ports were then plain `pipe`, which Py's pipes do not join.
- The limit of five crafts' worth, measured on one fast recipe and one test that fed ingredients by script.
- The first combinators built by script stood where no pole powers them: 129 of 1,247 deciders and 17 of 89 relief
  inserters sent and took nothing. They are placed only on powered tiles now.
- Demand built as "holds less than a craft's worth" counted every machine short all through each craft; the chlorine
  cells ran at 6.5 times their plan. The craft in hand counts as held now.
- 141 temporary game folders of stopped runs (109 GB) filled the disk at 02:30; every new run died until they were
  deleted.

One thing seen and not followed: the robot check's `--void-full` halves every product of a full machine, also those
far from their limit. With two crafts' worth as the limit for slow recipes, yesterday's deleted brains (33 in the
measured half of `n13_built`) may have been the blocking product after all; that is not settled either way.

## Verdict

**Does overflow control at the producer work?** Yes, as one part. A product that stops a machine is let out, at
that machine, while another product of it is still wanted. It is what keeps a multi-product machine from standing
(the flow rules without it stall at 12 packs), it can be read off the machine's own contents by decider
combinators, and built from real parts it carried the factory better than the script's blunt form (50 and 51 packs
in the measured half against 40). The limit to compare against is the game's own and differs by recipe speed; the
surplus must leave at the machine, not through a port that other makers share.

**Do circuit-controlled splitters help?** They exist in this game version, and nothing found tonight needs one. A
splitter cannot shut an output, and a gate at the end of a shared belt is the wrong place.

**Does it make belts work?** No. With every by-product looked after, the belt-built factory still makes 3 packs in
four hours, because no rule says who gets a shared belt's goods and which machines should be running at all. With
the check's pull and quotas on top it makes 82 to 105% of the planned packs in the measured half, with the check
handing in a third to a half of the slaughterhouse's animals, and it is running down all the while (a fifth of its
recipes off their rate by the last four hours, some at nothing). The robot-built factory made 97% with one stand-in
and 322 of 336 recipes at their rate.

**How far down the ladder did it get?** Of the four helps the working run needs, one is now a real part and proven
(relief), one is built and not yet proven (pull, 1,092 deciders), one has no part (quotas; rate caps did not replace
it), and one is a player's hand (loop carriers, here an ongoing one). A fifth fault was found on the way and is the
layout's: a maker that feeds two belts gives each half, and the lab instruments wait for rubber for most of a day.

**What I would do with this.** Belts are not hopeless, and they are not close. The honest comparison is a factory
that runs down under four helps against one that held 97% with one. I would not rebuild the planner around belt
logic. I would take two things from tonight into the planner whatever carries the items: relief at the machine as
the overflow rule (rules 10 and 10b of the robot design are two cases of the same thing, an overflow exit that
waits; that it can stand in for both is my reading, not tested; its chest needs robots or a port belt to empty it),
and an end to splitting by turns (the inserter toward a belt works while that belt is short; also untested alone).
And one run decides whether pull from real parts holds: with the carrier rule fixed, a night at most.

Not done: nothing was built into the planner; no block was laid out; the built pull run stood at 8 packs for a
reason of the check; the four cap runs were stopped before hour 16 (hours given above); the belt rule against
splitting by turns is not written.
