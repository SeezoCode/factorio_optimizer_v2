# Night log 2026-11-17: the belt factory with fair help only

The task: the belt-built Py logistic science factory (325 machines, laid out 2026-11-09) with only help that a
player could give or build, and real voids for what it destroys. Carriers once, as a kit; starters once; surplus
into the game's own void buildings. The flow rules themselves stay the check's script: a Lua combinator mod is to
carry them, so what counts is that each rule reads only what a wire at that place could read.

About 60 runs, six to ten at a time (one instance takes a little over one core; ten run at 4.5 times real time
each, six at about 6). Most were stopped as soon as their question was answered.

## In short

- **The belt factory does not yet hold on fair help alone.** It makes its packs at 1.4 to 1.7 times plan for five
  to twelve hours and then stops; no run on this factory held to its end. Every stop but one was traced to its
  cause, six in all, and each batch got past the one before: the check's quota rule locking the breeders out, the
  scrondrix loop planned with no margin, the exporters of filled barrels draining the water-barrel loop, the moss
  farm's catalyst leaving by an overflow port, the same catalyst piling up past a splitter, and a share rule too
  rigid for users that stray from the plan. (Not traced: one run whose water-barrel filler ran out of barrels.)
  The last change of the night (the share rule following demand) got past the stop it was made for and showed
  the next: while the breeders stand at their cap nobody is waiting, the rule lets go, and the renderer's meat goes
  by turns onto the belts of two users planned 0.9% of it between them (405 meat lying there three hours after the
  starters ended, the breeders short). The rule needs both halves, demand first and the plan's shares when nobody
  asks. That is not written.
- **Last night's 82% and 103% were worth less than they looked.** 44% of the water barrels in those runs were
  handed straight to the animals by the check, which covered a fault of the check's own (Py's barrel fillers were
  invisible to it while empty), and nearly half the scrondrix came in the same way. With carriers handed in only
  as a player would (empty, counted, in the warm-up) the same rules give packs on plan while the loop starters
  flow and nothing two hours after.
- **Three things wrong in every earlier belt run, now fixed in the check:**
  - the filler fault above;
  - **a maker with several belts shares out by turns** whatever the plan gave each (69 such cases; the water-barrel
    filler sent half its barrels to a belt planned 6%). A share rule at the output inserters doubles the packs of
    the first two hours and takes the barrels handed in from 3,041 to about 600;
  - **under pull with an overflow, a machine runs flat out for its smallest product** (the worm farm at 183 times
    plan, 17 to 20 items a second into the overflow). A cap of twice the plan per machine costs no packs and
    brings that to 1 a second.
- **Voids.** Fluids are done: the check now ends every fluid port in Py's valve and exhaust pipe or sinkhole (71 of
  75 ports fit; no power, no recipe to set), and a run with them matches one without. Items: the planner has a new
  option, `sinks.burn`, that plans Py's burners for every surplus item (31 burners here, 352 machines, laid out
  valid), and in the game they burn what arrives on coal from a belt. What is not solved is the plan around them:
  it makes things only to burn them, and those chains take the science's inputs. The overflow at the machines
  still ends in chests; with the cap it is 3,000 to 5,000 items an hour, and eight exits need a real burner.
- **Carriers as a kit hold.** 450 to 670 barrels and 20 to 140 bricks once, none while measured, and none lost in
  the runs I counted.
- **Starters: two to four hours' worth, in items a few chests** (bones above all, and the moss farm's catalyst).
  With none the factory does not start; with one hour the scrondrix loop has not gone round.
- **On the other factories:** Py science 1 (56 machines) under the same rules made 47 to 57 pack crafts an hour for
  16 hours against 24 due, with starters for two hours only. Py science 2 (406 machines) ran at half its plan from
  the start; I did not trace it. The logistic factory planned again with a 2% margin (337 machines) stood in its
  second hour.

**What I would do next**, in order: the share rule with both halves, and one run of this factory through 24
hours on it; then the planner, where three of the causes sit: a
margin on loops (or the loop's own users first), a catalyst's way back to its farm that does not pass on to other
users, and no chain made only to be burned. The rules themselves (share, cap, loop-first, pull, quotas over a
window) each read only their own machine, its inserters and the demand signal of its belts, so they fit a Lua
combinator.

## A fault in the check that the hand-ins covered

Py's barrel fillers are furnaces: they hold no recipe while empty. This layout is from before blueprints carried a
furnace's planned recipe, so an empty filler stood short of nothing in the check's eyes: it pulled no barrels from
the barrel maker, and the carrier rule could not see it wait. Nobody noticed, because the check handed filled water
barrels straight to the animals: 4,679 in the best run of 2026-11-16, 44% of all the water barrels the plan calls
for. With filled carriers no longer handed in, the filler made 38 water barrels in 2.3 hours of 661 an hour
planned. Fixed: a furnace-type machine with no planned recipe on record is planned for what it crafts. Every belt
run of the earlier nights has this fault in it, and the hand-ins that hid it.

## Carriers as a kit

`--carrier-kit`: the check hands in only the empty form of a carrier (barrel, cage, stone brick: the ones some
recipe makes from no other carrier), only to a machine that has waited two minutes for it, only during the
warm-up, and no overflow takes a carrier out of a machine. A caged scrondrix handed in was a free scrondrix: 520 of
them in eight measured hours on 2026-11-16, nearly half the animals.

- **Nothing leaks.** Barrels handed in plus barrels made equal the barrels in the factory, in every run looked at.
- **The number handed in depends on the split below.** Without the share rule the filler was given 3,041 barrels
  in eight hours, because half of what it filled lay unused on a belt; with it, 450 to 670.
- Bricks: 20 to 140. Cages: none.
- **The check's overflow ports took carriers too**, slowly, until I shut the ports of belts that carry one.

## One maker, several belts: the planned share

Last night's rubber loss was not special. A machine with inserters onto several belts shares out what it makes by
turns, whatever the plan gave each belt. The water-barrel filler has two: one onto the belt of the scrondrix (94% of
the planned use), one onto the belt of the cottonguts (6%). Half its barrels went each way: at hour 3.7 of the fair
run, 1,479 water barrels lay on the cottongut belt, the filler had made 159% of its plan, and the scrondrix cubs
stood short of water barrels at 74% of theirs. The breeding loop behind them ran at half its plan. 55 makers with
one item product drop onto two or more belt networks, and 14 more do so with one product of several (the
renderer's meat: three belts planned 99.1%, 0.6% and 0.3%).

- **`--belt-hold` (the wire to the belt, from last night's list) fails.** Built from real parts (368 of 387
  inserters wired to their belt tile, reading the whole belt, and 10 deciders for the rest): 6 pack crafts by hour
  1.7 against 13 without. What
  rests at the end of a belt past its last user counts as "the belt has enough", the inserter stops, and the user
  further up gets nothing more.
- **`--dead-end` (overflow ports of used items stay shut) does not do it either**: on plan for three hours, then
  nothing, 51 recipes standing.
- **`--out-share` works.** The inserters of a belt that is ahead of 1.2 times its planned share of what all of
  that maker's inserters carried are held, unless every belt behind its share is blocked. It reads nothing but its
  own inserters' hands. With it: 24 pack crafts by hour 1.9 against 13, 4 recipes under 85% of their plan against
  21, 292 water barrels on belts against 1,126. In a game: a counter per output inserter and a comparison.
- **But the plan's shares are too rigid when users stray from the plan**, and under these rules they do. In the two
  longest runs the titanium plates (two makers, a craft of 147 plates every five hours each) went to their belts by
  share: 867 lay on the belt of a user that was switched off, the bio-container maker on the other belt stood
  short, and the pack waited for batteries. Changed at 7:10: a belt behind its share counts only while a user on it
  waits for the item (the pull rule's own signal for that belt). That got the two last runs past hour 7 and has a
  hole of its own: a breeder switched off at its cap is not waiting, so while it is off the meat goes by turns to
  the small belts again.

## A machine may be run for its smallest product

Under pull a machine runs while some user waits for any of its products, and the overflow keeps it from standing
full. Together they let a machine run flat out for a by-product while its main product is destroyed. The worm
farm makes 18 worms and 1 powdered biomass a craft; a machine three steps on kept asking for powdered biomass, and
the farm ran at 183 times its plan, its worms going to the overflow at 6 a second. Washers ran at 100 to 270 times
plan for their sludge, the nexelit evaporator at 1,700 times. In an uncapped run 145 of the 173 recipes due two
crafts in an hour ran above 150% of plan, 112 above 300%, and the overflow took **17 to 20 items a second**; the
starters are not the cause (15 a second with none at all).

`--rate-cap 2` (no machine above twice its plan over ten minutes, plus three crafts) ends it: **0.7 to 1.3 items a
second** to the overflow, 23 recipes above 300% where there were 126, and more packs, not fewer (the earlier
nights' capped runs had all stopped on the split by turns; with the share rule they do not). The three crafts of
slack are too many for slow machines: one planned for a craft every two hours may still craft 18 times an hour.
`--meter 2` is the strict form (a token bucket of one craft).

## The loops are planned with no margin

With the hand-ins gone and the starters stopped, every run died within one to two hours, at the scrondrix loop.
Three causes, found one after the other:

- **The plan gives the loop nothing to grow on.** One scrondrix rendered gives 5 meat and, by way of its bones,
  3.3 more; breeding one takes 7.5. The 11% over is exactly what the animal samples and two small users are planned
  to take. Whatever is lost (a hand of meat down the wrong belt, bones taken by the overflow) is never grown back.
- **The check's quota rule locked the breeders out.** It counted what a machine took since the start of the run.
  A breeder that ran ahead while the starters flowed stayed "ahead of its quota" for hours afterwards and was held
  off its meat whenever any small user stood short: 1,583 meat lay on the breeders' belt with their inserters
  switched off. `--quota-window 1800` (an existing option) counts the last half hour instead.
- **A machine further down a loop ate its catalyst.** The moss farm keeps 15 coarse and gives 22 back; the next
  moss machine uses 15 a craft, ran at three times its plan (the cap's slack), and left the farm with 14.

`--loop-first`: a user of an item whose own products come back to a maker of that item within three recipes is
that item's loop (the farm itself counts as 0); any other user is held from the item while a user in the loop
stands short of it and took less than its plan in the last half hour. 13 items here (meat, bones, bonemeal,
coarse, barrel, fertilizer, ash, biomass, cottongut, fish, moondrop, seaweed, small lamp). "In a loop at all" says
nothing in this factory: 53 of its 313 recipes are one loop by way of ash, biomass and barrels.

What the rule did and did not do: the breeders kept their meat (the scrondrix loop ran at 150% of plan four hours
after the starters ended, where it had been dead within one), and the exporters of filled barrels stopped taking
the water-barrel loop's barrels (that had ended the longest run at hour 13: the barrel maker is planned for exactly
what the exporters take, they ran at 136%, and the loop's 1,100 barrels were down to 478). It did not save the
moss farm, and there the cause is the layout: the farm's catalyst returns by a belt past a splitter that also
leads on to the next moss machine, the farm's own inserter catches what it can off the splitter, and what it
misses piles up on the branch beyond (73 coarse there, the farm at 13 of the 15 it needs, the machine at the end of
the branch held by the rule). Nothing brings it back. The same belt's overflow port had let the catalyst out of the
factory before I shut the ports of loop items and carriers (17 of 123 ports).

## Starters

- **With none at all the factory does not start**: 4 pack crafts and then nothing. Only the scrondrix loop is
  short; everything else behind is downstream of it.
- What the starters give in items is small: 1,300 to 8,300 bones in the first two hours (by the cap or not), a few
  hundred pure sand, coke, seaweed, cottongut pups, 56 coarse. A chest or two.
- **In fluids they are not a kit.** Any machine short of a seeded fluid has its box filled every two seconds:
  2.9 million tailings, 560,000 methanol, 430,000 sulfuric acid in two hours of an uncapped run. With the cap:
  21,000 steam and a few thousand of the rest.
- **How long they must run** (with the cap, so in items a few chests): one hour is too little, the scrondrix loop
  has not gone round. Two hours carry the scrondrix loop (alive at 150% of plan four hours later) but leave the
  moss farm's belt unfilled (above). Four fill it: 157 coarse on the belt, the farm at 23 crafts and running.
- **Starters do not explain every stop.** The two runs with starters to hour 6 and to hour 8 were alike to the
  craft for five hours and both stopped in hour 7, one with its starters still flowing.

## Voids

### Fluids: real, and cheap

`--real-voids` puts Py's overflow valve and an exhaust pipe or sinkhole on every fluid output port of the test
copy, in place of its infinity pipe: 71 of this factory's 75 ports (30 sinkholes, 26 exhaust pipes straight out of
the port, 15 more behind up to six extra pipes; 4 found no room). They need no power and no recipe. A run with them
matches the same run without: 47 pack crafts by hour 7.2 against 49. In those seven hours of an uncapped run they
destroyed 8.6 million steam, 1.9 million refinery syngas and 1.5 million water; with the cap, 80,000 blood and
pitch and 60,000 steam in nine hours.

In the planner the same buildings already existed for small by-products (`blocks.local_sinks`). New: fluids the
program renamed (`steam#250`, `water#byproduct`) get them too; the buildings take a fluid at any temperature.

### Items: planned surplus into burners

`sinks.burn` (new, in the planner): an item named there (`"*"`: every item) may not leave the factory as a
by-product; what is left over goes to Py's burner, planned like any machine with its coal by belt and its ash to
the ash users or burned in turn. This factory with every item burned and the small fluids vented: **352 machines
instead of 325, 31 burners for 2,700 items an hour, 42 sinkholes, 10 exhaust pipes, 91 valves**, valid, 369 x 445
tiles (stopped at the first factory; the old one is 290 x 346). One item port is left besides the pack's: fuel
items and filled barrels, which Py's burner has no recipe for (coal dust at 614 an hour is most of it).

In the game the burners work: all 31 fuelled by belt, burning what arrives, 29 caught up at any moment. Three
things showed:

- **The check's pull rule switched them off** (nobody waits for ash) **and then counted them as users** (they ran
  their makers at the cap). Both fixed: a machine that only destroys is never switched off and waits for nothing.
- **Two burners stood full of their own ash** (1,000 in the output): what they burn arrived at thousands of times
  its plan, and their ash belt is sized for the plan.
- **The plan has chains that exist only to feed burners**, and in the game they compete with the science for
  their inputs: blood is made into albumin to burn (1,920 of 2,520 blood an hour; the animal samples, planned 600,
  stood short of it), and three fungal substrates are made from petri-dish bacteria and burned while the plasmids
  wait for bacteria. That run made 7 pack crafts in 3.4 hours. Pricing vented fluids at a hundredth made it worse
  (593 machines; reverted). The planner must not turn a surplus into something else to burn; that is the next
  thing to do there.

## Measured in the game (Factorio 2.1.24, Py), on a spare surface

- **Py's overflow valve opens at 80% full.** A pipe held at 100% or 90% by its source ran through the valve and stood
  at 80 of 100 before it; one held at 70% let nothing through. About 3,500 a second went through one valve, 2,350
  with a void building straight behind it.
- **The exhaust pipe (gases, 1x1) and the sinkhole (liquids, 3x3) need no power and no recipe set.** They are
  furnaces: water, syngas and steam each picked their own void recipe on arrival, at any temperature (steam at 250
  and 500, outlet gas at 1000, water at 90). 20,000 of the fluid a craft, a craft in half a second.
- **The burner (3x3) destroys 3.3 items a second, whatever the item**, on one coal for every 67 items, and leaves a
  fifth of an ash for each item plus the ash of its coal. One input slot, one fuel slot, one output slot (it stands
  at 1,000 ash), one slot for burnt fuel. Rubber, worms, cages and ash itself all burned at the same rate; a plain
  inserter kept it fed.
- **Py has no burner recipe for items that are fuel** (coal dust, logs, carbon black, kerogen: they go in the fuel
  slot) **or for filled barrels** (2,613 void recipes for 3,300 items).
- A belt tile set to read the whole belt (hold) gives every item on its line; an inserter wired to it with an
  enable condition stops as the count reaches the limit.

## What did not work, or made things worse

- `--belt-hold` and `--dead-end`: above.
- `--virtual-pumps` (a pump in front of every fluid input): 19 pack crafts by hour 5.8 and none in the last three
  hours. Every short machine pulls the pipe down, and the lens maker stood without hot air while its maker ran at
  257% of plan.
- Laying the burner factory out with every by-product fluid vented inside it (`blocks.local_sinks` at any rate):
  one block found no layout in twelve minutes on four threads.

## Code

Check (`tools/factorio-verify`): `--carrier-kit`, `--seed-stop`, `--real-voids`, `--belt-hold`, `--out-share`,
`--loop-first`; furnace-type machines learn their recipe; void machines exempt from pull; the relief exits' chests
counted by item and recipe; carriers handed in by the hour in the timeline. Planner: `sinks.burn`, voids for
renamed fluids, a unit test for each. All committed, nothing pushed.
