## Why every run stops: nothing queues at the users

Last night I traced six stops to six causes. Tonight's were one cause, seen on four layouts: **a user without an
item, its maker stopped or held, and the item lying on that user's own belt, past it.**

| Where | The user short | Lying past it on its own belt |
|---|---|---:|
| old layout, water barrels | the scrondrix cubs, 8 short | 273 |
| margin layout, filtration media | the trit breeders, without any | 254 |
| Py science 1, seaweed (strict loop rule) | the fertilizer maker, 2 short | 146 |
| Py science 1, seaweed (slow machines held) | the slaked-lime maker, 1 short | 120 |

Items on a belt reach a user only as they pass, or once the queue from the belt's end stands at its inserter. These
layouts have almost no splitters (9 in 23,566 entities): a belt runs past its users one after the other and ends at
the last one or at an overflow port. Pull and the caps keep the belts lean on purpose, so nothing queues, and what a
user does not need at the moment it passes goes on and parks at the far end. These are trickle flows on express
belts: a belt network of a few hundred tiles holds a few thousand items, the planned flow of filtration media is 72
an hour, so "let the belts fill up" is tens of hours for one item.

For an ordinary item the maker makes more and the next batch passes by; that works as long as makers may run well
above plan. In a loop the maker depends on that user, and the loop stands for good. Py science 1's seaweed loop
gives 15 seaweed for 1; it died with 146 seaweed in sight of the machine that needed 2.

That is also what the earlier nights' numbers rested on. Every rule that made runs "hold" (starters for hours, three
crafts over the cap, two over a quota) kept items passing by more often; every rule that made the factory keep to
its plan took that away.

## A reserve at the user: the one rule aimed at the cause

`--reserve N` (with `--loop-first`): per loop item and belt network, the first plain belt tile past the last of the
loop's own users is a gate that stays shut until a lane of the tile N back up the belt takes nothing more. N tiles
of queue stand at the loop's users before anything goes on to the other users or the belt's end. The gate opens
for whatever fills the belt, so a mixed belt cannot lock. In a game: one wire from the tile N back (read contents)
to the gate tile's enable condition. It took four attempts to get right (a gate at each user starved the users below
it; a user below a shut gate was never seen taking the item, so the gate never moved; "four items on a lane" is
never true on a curve, whose inner lane holds two).

**On Py science 1 it is the difference between a stop and a day's run.** One gate, past the fertilizer maker on
the seaweed loop's belt:

- 24 hours, 1,161 pack crafts where 576 are due, every hour from the fourth on between 46 and 50. Nothing handed in
  after hour 2; all the starters of the run were 683 seaweed, 17 coarse, 200 methanol and 80 phytoplankton, and no
  carriers. The seaweed went 89.2% and 10.8% to belts planned 89.3% and 10.7% (24,804 and 2,991), and the loop-first
  rule did not have to hold anyone once in the 24 hours.
- Those 1,161 are twice the plan because the exit at the pack machine was taking every second pack (a fault of
  the check, below). With that exit gone the port takes packs at the planned rate and the factory makes exactly
  that: **24 pack crafts in every one of 23 hours, 0.02 packs a second at the port over the 22 measured hours**,
  on 577 seaweed, 30 coarse, 200 methanol and 80 phytoplankton handed in during the first two hours. The exits took
  47 items an hour (iron oxide and coke).
- Without the gate the same factory stopped at hour 4, 5, 6 or 10 in six runs (table below).

`--reserve-all` puts such a gate past every user's pickup tile for every item (181 gates on the margin layout, where
most users sit at their belt's end and need none). On Py science 1 (8 gates) it ran like the loop-only
gate for 13 hours. On the margin layout under the plain rules it made 6 4 2 0 pack crafts in its first four hours
against 12 7 6 3 without, and was stopped; why it costs there I did not look into.

## The share rule, both halves

`--out-share-mode both` (the default now). Per item a machine puts onto several belt networks:

- a network with a user waiting for the item (short of it, and not held from it by a quota or the loop rule) takes
  it; among those, one ahead of its share is held while another that waits is behind its share and has room;
- a network nobody waits on is held while a waiting one has room, and otherwise while it is ahead of its share and
  one behind its share has room.

Kept as a balance per network (its share of what all carried, less what it carried), never above four times the
slack. Three things I had wrong on the way, each found in a run:

- **Counts that fade.** My first form let them fade with a half-life; that hands every small belt its slack again
  each half hour, 5% of the renderer's meat. Gone before any run finished.
- **A slack of two crafts' worth.** For a recipe that makes 200 at a craft that is the whole batch: the margin
  layout's filtration media went a quarter to each of four belts. The slack is one inserter's hand now (16).
- **A user counted on every belt it takes anything from.** A machine with five input belts put its planned use, and
  its waiting, on all five. The check now learns from the inserters' hands which belt brings which item and counts
  a user there only. With both fixes the trit breeders' belt got 275 of the first 400 media (69%, planned 82%) where
  it had got 99.

On the old layout the renderer's meat went 93.7%, 5.7% and 0.6% to belts planned 99.0%, 0.6% and 0.3% (4,322, 264
and 28 in 5.6 hours), where last night's rule had left 405 lying on the two small belts; the water barrels 90% and
10% against 94% and 6%. The 5.7% is not the rule's: the user on that belt ran at seven times its plan.

`strict` (a network nobody waits on stays held even when every other belt is blocked) ran within a craft of `both`
for three hours and was stopped.

## What did not work

- **Slow machines held to their plan** (`--cap-slow`). The cap allows a machine twice its plan over ten minutes plus
  three crafts, a quota one and a half times over half an hour plus two. For a machine planned for a craft or two an
  hour the crafts "over" are the whole allowance, and such machines do run at many times their plan and take what a
  large user was planned for: powdered quartz, planned 2 crafts an hour, made 73 in two hours and took half the
  crushed quartz the glass is planned 96% of; Py science 2's arqad queen, planned 14% of the natural gas barrels, ran
  at 628% while the arqads planned 86% got a quarter of theirs. Counting such machines over the time three planned
  crafts take, with one craft over, stops that and starves the factory: the loops stop running rich, and nothing
  passes the users any more (table above). With a leave for a capped machine to run on for a user that is itself
  behind plan (`--cap-fill`) it lasted as long as without either. Both are in the check and the mod, off.
- **The loop rule made strict** (`--loop-strict`: the other users wait whenever a loop user stands short, not only
  while it is behind its plan). Written for the burner layout, whose scrondrix loop ran at 170% on its starters and
  was drained in the half hour after they ended. On Py science 1 it holds the agar (89% of the seaweed's planned
  use) off the seaweed for good once the loop's stock is parked.
- **No pull at all, every overflow port shut**, so belts fill and back-pressure stops the makers, with shares,
  quotas, loop-first and the exits kept: 5 pack crafts in 0.8 hours where pull makes 57 in the first hour. With every
  machine running flat out the quotas stop the fluid users in turn, and the fluid ports, which cannot be shut,
  vented 280,000 steam and 200,000 hydrogen in that time.
- **Every overflow port shut, with pull** (`--dead-end`): fine for three hours on the old layout, dead in the second
  hour on Py science 1.
- **Starters as one delivery** (`--seed-kit 40`): 4 pack crafts and nothing. That kit put 195 bones into
  the factory; with starters for four hours the loop took 2,200.

## The rules in the mod, and the player's blueprint

`tools/factory-flow` (the project's own mod, version 0.2.0) now carries loop-first, the share rule, the shut ports
of loop items and carriers, the reserve gates, the slow-machine rule and its leave (both off), and it finds the
recipe of a furnace the blueprint names none for. `tag.py` writes the loop tags and, with `--built`, adds to the
blueprint what the check builds from real parts: the overflow exits (inserter, chest, deciders, wires) and the fluid
voids (valve, exhaust pipe or sinkhole, pipes). The check writes those out with `--export-built` and tests such a
blueprint with `--flow-mod --with-built`.

- **The blueprints with everything in them build in the game**: the old layout 18,835 entities, all 149 deciders
  wired, 89 exits, 71 fluid voids; the margin layout 24,782 entities, 168 deciders, 93 exits, 121 fluid voids. The
  margin layout's is `solves/py_logi_margin/flow_blueprint.txt` (not in git, like every layout).
- **The mod does what the check's script does.** On the old layout after six minutes: 285 machines switched off and
  51 capped under the mod, 282 and 56 under the script; by the hour 10 10 12 9 6 pack crafts against 10 10 11 11 7.
  On Py science 1, with the blueprint bringing its own exits and voids and the mod its reserve gate: 24 pack crafts
  in every hour of a 24-hour run and 0.02 packs a second at the port, the script's numbers exactly. Without the
  reserve gate the mod's run stopped at hour 4, as the script's did at hour 10.
- **Every earlier `--flow-mod` run used the mod's defaults.** The check wrote the mod's settings from its own mod,
  which the game refuses without an error (only the owning mod may write a setting). The rate cap, the quota window
  and the stock were never set; the mod now sets them itself on the check's request, and the check warns when a
  setting did not take.
- **The overflow exit at the pack machine took packs.** An exit takes a product at its limit; nothing excepted the
  target. On Py science 1 the exit's chest got 69 packs an hour, as many as reached the port. Excepted now, and the
  exported blueprints are without that exit.
