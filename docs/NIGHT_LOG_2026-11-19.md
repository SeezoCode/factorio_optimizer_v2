# Night log 2026-11-19: repeats of the belt factory, and what stops it

The task: work through [BELT_FACTORY_PLAN.md](BELT_FACTORY_PLAN.md) overnight, until 7:00. The plan's first step was
repeats: the rule set that ran logistic science's margin layout 20 hours once, run several times over, with and
without the reserve gates and under the mod, to learn whether it holds or was lucky.

Hours are game hours. Twelve games at once on ten cores ran the big layout at about 2.5 times real time, so no run
of it reached 24 hours by morning; they were read from samples taken every few game minutes (where each loop item
lies, on which belt, in which machine, and every loop machine's state and crafts).

## In short

- **Logistic science's rule set holds about one run in three, not every time.** Nine repeats of the rule set that
  ran 20 hours last night: three were still making packs at hour 16 to 17 when stopped, six had stopped, five of
  them within four hours of the starters ending. Gates or no gates made no clear difference (one in four, one in
  three, and one in two under the mod).
- **Every stop was a loop that ran short once and never restarted**, and there are four ways it happened: fish
  parked past the last loop user on 40 tiles of dead-end belt; a loop user switched off by pull, so nothing counted
  it as waiting while its stock went elsewhere (seaweed farm, moss maker); a maker sharing out by the plan while
  a loop user waited (the fertilizer maker gave the seaweed farm's belt its planned 15%); and a loop of fluids
  (syngas), which loop priority did not know.
- **A rule aimed at the second cause looks promising**: with `--loop-keep 2` (a loop user waits for its item below
  two crafts' worth, switched off or not), none of four runs was lost to an item loop by hour 12, against five of
  nine without it. Four runs and twelve hours: suggestive, not proven. Rules for the third and fourth causes are
  written and ran a few hours without a stop.
- **Py science 1 runs on its planned inputs**: with every raw input capped at the plan's rate it slumps for six
  hours after the starters and then makes its 24 pack crafts an hour to hour 24.
- **Py science 1's starter kit is small**: 150 seaweed, 18 coarse, 200 methanol and 80 phytoplankton carried it
  24 hours at plan.
- **The check's verdict is mended**: runs that held now pass (held inserters and fluid voids are no fault), and a
  run that stood at hour 11 now fails (the port read 100% for hours after the last craft; the verdict now also
  asks that the pack machine kept on over the last quarter).

## Why they stop

Every stop was traced from the samples back to where the loop first went short, not from its end state. Three
causes, all loops, all early (hour 3 to 4, one to two hours after the starters):

- **Fish parked past the egg breeder** (both runs without gates that stopped, and one mod run with gates). The egg
  breeder takes fish from a belt that goes on past it through two long undergrounds, about 40 tiles, to a port that
  is shut for loop items. What passes the breeder while it is full parks there: 150 to 160 fish, with the breeder
  empty (the 36 fish it shows are its module slots) and the fish breeders each half a craft short of eggs. The
  reserve gate sits just past the breeder and holds a queue there, but it opens whenever that queue is full, and the
  surplus then parks the same way. In the mod run the gate was in place and the fish still parked. A surplus that
  parks is lost to a loop planned with 9% to spare (125 fish an hour made, 115 bred back, 10 for three other users).
- **Seaweed: a loop user switched off is not counted as waiting** (one gate run). The fertilizer maker ran at
  twice its plan for an hour while its users were full and put 637 fertilizer onto the moondrop breeders' belt; the
  seaweed farm was switched off by pull meanwhile, so the share rule saw nobody waiting on its belt and its
  fertilizer ran out there. The fertilizer maker had eaten the seaweed the slaked-lime maker beside it needed, and
  the farm needs slaked lime (a fluid) as well as fertilizer: the farm stood, and 1,728 seaweed lay parked on the
  belt of the users outside the loop.
- **Syngas, a loop of fluids** (one run of the new rule below). Syngas goes round by way of refined syngas and
  oleochemicals; the plastic maker and four other users outside the loop take it from the same pipes, and loop
  priority knew items only. The loop's own user stood short of syngas, then everything behind it.

- **Coarse, a loop that is one machine** (one gate run, at hour 15). The moss maker takes 15 coarse to make about
  17; the plan leaves it 5.7 coarse an hour over for the other moss recipe, which takes 5.4. Pull kept the moss maker
  switched off nearly all the time, so loop priority (which counts a loop user only while it is switched on) never
  held the other recipe back, and the coarse went to it a craft at a time. At hour 13 the maker held 13 of the 15
  it needs, the other held 9, and nothing else makes coarse.
- **Seaweed again, at hour 10** (one more gate run). The same as the first, and plainer: both belts the fertilizer
  maker feeds had a user waiting, so it shared out by the plan, and the plan gives the seaweed farm's belt 15%.
  Its last 24 crafts went mostly onto the moondrop breeders' belt (405 lying there afterwards), the farm's belt ran
  dry, and the last seaweed had gone into that fertilizer instead of to the slaked-lime maker.

So the gate is not what decides: one of four gate runs stopped at hour 3, one of three runs without gates is still
going. What the runs that stop have in common is a loop that runs short once, at a moment its own users are switched
off or its stock lies out of reach, and never restarts.

## The check's verdict

The verdict line failed every run, the ones that held included: it counted an inserter switched off by the flow
rules with an item in its hand as stuck, and Py's exhaust pipes and sinkholes as machines without a recipe. Both
are no fault and are now left out. It also passed a run that had stood since hour 11: the port reads its planned
rate for hours after the last craft, because the pack machine ran at twice its plan and filled the belt to the port.
The verdict now also asks for 80% of the target makers' planned crafts over the last quarter of the measured time.
Judged again: the 20-hour run, the 14-hour mod run and three Py science 1 runs pass; the run that stood at hour 11
fails ("0 crafts in the last 5.0 hours, planned 30.6").

## Inputs at the plan's rate

Every run until tonight had unlimited raw inputs. `--input-cap F` now gives each raw input belt at most F times its
planned rate (fluids stay unlimited), with at most a minute's worth saved up. On Py science 1, 24 hours, the rule
set that holds it (`--rate-cap 2 --reserve 4`):

| Inputs | Pack crafts by hour (24 planned) | At the port over 22 hours |
|---|---|---|
| unlimited | 24 in every hour | 100% |
| 1.2 times the plan | 35 32 15 12 5 6 15 18 26 30 28 29 29 27, then 24 every hour | 90% |
| the plan | 30 28 5 10 12 5 9 11 5, then 24 every hour (one 22) | 80% |

So the plan's own inputs carry the factory once it has settled: from hour 9 on it made its 24 pack crafts an hour
on exactly what the plan says it needs. The slump of hours 3 to 9 is the belts filling: while the starters flowed
the factory ran at up to twice its plan and emptied its belts of everything the starters did not cover, and at the
plan's rate nothing is left over to fill them again quickly. In the slump the fertilizer maker stood 17 biomass
short with 236 biomass in the long undergrounds of its own belt.

A gate past the last user of every item (`--reserve-items`), meant for that, did worse: the seaweed loop died at
hour 17. A stone gate on the belt that also carries the seaweed had taken the seaweed gate's tile.

## Rules tried tonight

All in `tools/factorio-verify` (the check), not yet in the mod.

| Option | What it does | Aimed at | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| `--loop-keep K` | a loop's own user counts as waiting for the loop's item while it holds less than K crafts' worth, switched off by pull or not: pull wakes the item's makers, the share rule sends to its belt, the other users wait | seaweed farm and moss maker switched off while their stock went elsewhere | 0 of 4 runs lost to an item loop by hour 12 (base: 5 of 9) |
| `--loop-share` | where a maker feeds several belts, one with a loop user waiting comes before every other, whatever the plan's shares | the fertilizer maker sharing out by plan while the farm waited | 2 of 2 alive at hour 5.8: too short to tell |
| `--loop-fluids` | fluid loops are found too (fluid inputs counted in the loop graph); a fluid's users outside its loop are switched off while a loop user stands short of it | syngas | 1 of 1 alive at hour 7.7: too short to tell. It found 26 loop fluids, water and steam among them; fluids that an input pipe brings are now left out (24 remain, steam still among them): still wider than meant |
| `--reserve-items` | a gate past the last user of every item | parked biomass at capped inputs | worse (seaweed loop dead at hour 17 on Py science 1) |
| `--input-cap F` | raw input belts at F times the plan | | see above |

Tried and dropped before running: ranking a loop's users by their distance back to the item's maker, so the nearer
ones come first. The slaked-lime maker looked two steps from the seaweed farm and the fertilizer maker one, but
slaked lime goes to the farm as a fluid, which the distance count left out. Both are one step away, and ranking them
would have starved a loop input.

## What this changes in the plan

- Step 1 (repeats) is answered: the rule set is not finished. It holds about one run in three.
- Every stop is a loop that runs short once and never restarts, and the four ways it happens are now known (parked
  past the last user; a loop user switched off and so not counted; a maker sharing out by plan while a loop user
  waits; fluid loops with no priority). The next runs should be `--loop-keep 2 --loop-share --loop-fluids` together,
  eight times over, 24 hours, with the fluid loops narrowed to fluids some recipe makes from the loop.
- The fish stops are parking past the last loop user, which no rule of flow can mend once the item has passed. The
  layout has to end the belt at the egg breeder or bring what passes back round; the gate holds a queue but lets
  the surplus go to a dead end. That is planner work (step 5 of the plan).
- Inputs at the plan's rate are enough for Py science 1 once its belts have filled, and a starter kit of a tenth of
  an hour's use carries it 24 hours. Both are still to be measured on logistic science.
