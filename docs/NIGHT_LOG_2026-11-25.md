## Splitter priority

The layout has 19 splitters: 17 where a belt branches toward two groups of users, two dead-end stubs. An even split
sends half of a short item down the side whose users are full, where it parks. `--splitter-priority` (check) and the
mod's `splitter-priority` setting look at each branching splitter every second: priority to the side where a machine
waits for what the belt carries (2 points for a machine inside the item's loop, 1 for any), none on a tie. A player
builds the same by wiring the splitter (2.x splitters take their priority from a signal).

Most of the changes are at the advanced circuit and pack splitters near the target, about 4,000 switches per run in
the first two and a half hours. One branch there has no users on one side; with priority the circuits stop going
down it.

## The plutonium lock

Traced after d4, e1 and e3 all stopped at hour 5 with the pack short of nuclear samples:

- `pu-241-transmutation`: 40 pu-241 and 80 neutron in, 24 pu-241, 12 pu-242 and 4 am-241 out. The output inserter
  drops pu-241 back onto the two-tile belt its input inserter takes from.
- In every run it stood with 51 to 54 pu-241 in its output and 14 to 20 in its input, status full output, from its
  sixth craft on (6 of 73 planned crafts by hour 5.6). A machine stops taking ingredients once two crafts' worth of a
  product lies in its output (measured 2026-11-16); its only exit for pu-241 is the belt its input takes from, which
  then never drains.
- First guess, the warm start's script top-ups (they pass the output limit): `--seed-own-wait` stops topping up an
  item the machine gives back while its output holds some. It did not help: f1 to f3 locked at the same sixth craft.
- Taking 7 pu-241 out of the output by hand in e1: 16 crafts in the next three minutes. Without help, it locked again
  after 19 more crafts. So it is the layout: a return belt that holds less than one craft's return.
- `--relief-own-jam`: when a machine stands full on a product it also takes, with less than a craft of it in its
  input, its exit lets that product out until the output is under the limit. The g runs lost 10 to 23 pu-241 to it
  by hour 1.6, and the machine ran at plan (17 to 24 crafts against 20).

A player cannot build this with a decider on the machine: reading a machine's contents adds input and output
together, and the lock (51 out, 20 in) reads like normal running. The fix belongs in the router: a catalyst's
return belt must hold two crafts' return, or the return must go onto both lanes.
